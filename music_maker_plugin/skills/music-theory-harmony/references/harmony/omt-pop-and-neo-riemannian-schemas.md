## 1. Summary of Common Pop Harmonic Schemas

| Schema | Roman Numerals | C Major / Minor Example | Common Variations |
|---|---|---|---|
| **12-Bar Blues** | `I \| I \| I \| I \| IV \| IV \| I \| I \| V \| IV \| I \| I` | `C \| C \| C \| C \| F \| F \| C \| C \| G \| F \| C \| C` | Dominant 7ths on every chord; 16-bar blues; `IV` in m. 2; `ii–V` in mm. 13–14 / applied `ii–V` in m. 8 (jazz blues); turnaround in mm. 15–16 |
| **Double Plagal** | `♭VII–IV–I` | `B♭–F–C` | Rotation: `I–♭VII–IV` |
| **Extended Plagal** | `♭VI–♭III–♭VII–IV–I` | `A♭–E♭–B♭–F–C` | Descending-fourths chain of applied `IV` chords |
| **Doo-Wop** | `I–vi–IV–V` | `C–Am–F–G` | Substitute `ii` for `IV` (`I–vi–ii–V`); rotation (`IV–V–I–vi`) |
| **Singer/Songwriter** | `vi–IV–I–V` (or `I–V–vi–IV`) | `Am–F–C–G` (or `C–G–Am–F`) | Any rotation (`IV–I–V–vi`, `V–vi–IV–I`); relative minor reading `i–VI–III–VII` |
| **Hopscotch** | `IV–V–vi–I` (or `VI–VII–i–III`) | `F–G–Am–C` | Replace `VII` with `V` in minor (`VI–V–i–III`); invert `V` to preserve step-step-skip bass |
| **Lament** | `i–♭VII–♭VI–V` | `Cm–B♭–A♭–G` | Minor `v` instead of `V`; major `I`; passing chords (`V6` between `i` and `♭VII`, `IV6` between `♭VII` and `♭VI`) |
| **Circle-of-Fifths** | `i–iv–VII–III` (etc.) | `Cm–Fm–B♭–E♭` | Extended fifthwise root motion; chromatic applied `ii–V` pairs |
| **Puff** | `I–iii–IV` | `C–Em–F` | Minor version `i–III–iv` (or `i–III–IV`); deceptive chromatic `I–III♯–IV` |
| **Subtonic Shuttle** | `I–♭VII` / `i–♭VII` | `C–B♭` / `Cm–B♭` | Rotation: `♭VII–I` |
| **Aeolian Shuttle** | `i–♭VII–♭VI–♭VII` | `Cm–B♭–A♭–B♭` | Shuttle between `i` and `♭VI` via passing `♭VII` |
| **Aeolian Cadence** | `♭VI–♭VII–i` | `A♭–B♭–Cm` | Picardy third (`♭VI–♭VII–I`, the "Mario cadence" / heroic fanfare) |
| **Dorian Shuttle** | `i–IV` | `Cm–F` | Expanded to `i–IV–i7–IV` |
| **Lydian Shuttle** | `I–II♯` | `C–D` | Often neutralized later by `IV` or `ii` |
| **Lydian Cadence** | `II♯–IV–I` | `D–F–C` | Smooth chromatic descent `♯4 → ♮4 → 3` (`fi–fa–mi`) |

## 2. Schema Families & Voice-Leading Mechanics

### Blues-Based & Plagal Schemas ("Flat-Side" Harmony)
- **I–IV Vamp**: Common in soul/R&B (`"Soul Man"`, `"In the Midnight Hour"`), often paired with 5–6 shuffle motion.
- **Minor `iv` / "Plagal Sigh" (`IV–iv–I`)**: Features chromatic descending line **`la–le–sol` (`^6–♭^6–^5`)** into tonic, evoking nostalgia and sentimentality.
- **Double & Extended Plagal**: Uses `♭VII` as `IV/IV` (`♭VII–IV–I`), or chains descending-fourths applied `IV` chords (`♭VI–♭III–♭VII–IV–I`).

### Four-Chord Schemas (`I, IV, V, vi`)
- Distinguishable by **how major tonic `I` is approached** (even under rotation):
  - **Doo-Wop (`I–vi–IV–V`)**: Approaches `I` by **authentic motion (`V → I`)**.
  - **Singer/Songwriter (`vi–IV–I–V` / `I–V–vi–IV`)**: Approaches `I` by **plagal motion (`IV → I`)**. Exhibits strong **tonal ambiguity** between relative major and relative minor (`vi–IV–I–V` vs. `i–VI–III–VII`), usable to link minor verses to major choruses.
  - **Hopscotch (`IV–V–vi–I`)**: Approaches `I` by **a skip (`vi → I`)** following a step-step-skip root motion (`IV–V–vi–I`). Common post-2010 pop schema; replacing `VII` with major `V` (often inverted to keep the step-step-skip bass) sharpens minor-mode focus.

### Classical Schemas in Pop
- **Lament (`i–♭VII–♭VI–V`)**: Bass descends the minor tetrachord `do–te–le–sol` (`^1–♭^7–♭^6–^5`). Syncopating middle chords leaves room for dominant `V7` turnaround.
- **Circle-of-Fifths**: Root motion down by fifth (`i–iv–VII–III` etc.). Naturally pivots from minor `i` to relative major `III` (`VII–III` heard as `V–I`).

### Puff Schemas (`I–iii–IV`)
- Used at the **opening of goal-oriented phrases** rather than endless 4-chord loops.
- Chromatic **`I–III♯–IV`** raises the third of `iii`, producing a rising chromatic inner line **`sol–si–la` (`^5–♯^5–^6`)**, heard as a deceptive resolution of `V/vi` (`III♯ → IV`) rather than `V/vi → vi`.

### Modal Schemas & Color Notes
A modal passage is defined by its **characteristic "color note"** relative to major (for major-third tonics: Major, Mixolydian, Lydian) or natural minor (for minor-third tonics: Aeolian, Dorian, Phrygian):
- **Mixolydian**: Major tonic + lowered `^7` (`te` / `♭^7`) → `♭VII` (dominant-functioning substitute for `V`). Schemas: `♭VII–IV–I`, `I–♭VII`.
- **Aeolian**: Minor tonic + `le` (`♭^6`) and `te` (`♭^7`) → `♭VI` (subdominant) and `♭VII` (dominant), avoiding leading-tone `ti`. Schemas: `i–♭VII`, `i–♭VII–♭VI–♭VII`, `♭VI–♭VII–i` (or `♭VI–♭VII–I`), `i–♭VII–♭VI–v`.
- **Dorian**: Minor tonic + raised `^6` (`la` / `♮^6`) → **major `IV` chord** (the primary marker of Dorian in funk, disco, and soul). Schema: `i–IV` or `i–IV–i7–IV`.
- **Lydian**: Major tonic + raised `^4` (`fi` / `♯^4`) → **major `II♯` chord** (when it does not resolve to `V` as `V/V`). Schemas: `I–II♯`, `II♯–IV–I` (where `IV` immediately neutralizes `fi` back to `fa`).

**Three-Step Ear/Mode Identification:**
1. Identify tonic chord quality (major `mi` vs. minor `me`).
2. Check scale degree `^7`: lowered `te` on a major tonic → Mixolydian; raised `ti` on a minor tonic → harmonic/tonal minor.
3. Check raised color notes: raised `^4` (`fi`) in major → Lydian; raised `^6` (`la`) in minor → Dorian; neither → Major or Aeolian.

### Pentatonic Harmony
- Major and minor pentatonic scales contain **no semitones**.
- In rock/guitar-driven music, pentatonic scale degrees are used directly as roots of **major triads or power chords** (`1, ♭3, 4, 5, ♭7` or `1, 2, 3, 5, 6`), producing chord families that mix `te`/`ti` or `le`/`la` outside single diatonic modes.

## 3. Fragile, Absent, and Emergent Tonics (Spicer 2017)

1. **Fragile Tonic**: The tonic chord appears, but is **weakened** (e.g., kept in **first inversion** instead of root position, or destabilized by surrounding inversions), evoking vulnerability or sensitivity.
2. **Emergent Tonic**: The true tonic chord is **withheld during intro/verse/prechorus** (often sounding in the relative minor or another provisional center) and **revealed triumphantly at the chorus**, creating dramatic payoff.
3. **Absent Tonic**: The tonic chord **never appears in the harmony at all** (e.g., looping `IV–ii–vi–V`), yet is unmistakably implied by the melody (`mi–re–do` / `^3–^2–^1`) and conventional harmonic syntax.
