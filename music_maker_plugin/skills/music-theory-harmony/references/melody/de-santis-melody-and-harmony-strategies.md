# Melodic & Harmonic Producer Strategies (Dennis DeSantis, *Making Music*)

*Distilled faithfully from Dennis DeSantis, "Making Music: 74 Creative Strategies for Electronic Music Producers" (Part 2: Problems of Progressing, pp. 178–237).*

## 1. Melodic Craft

### Creating Melodies 1: Contour (pp. 178–181)
- **Contour** is the shape of pitch movement over time (rising, falling, staying static) and the balance between **conjunct motion** (stepwise, smooth, singable) and **disjunct motion** (leaps, dramatic, angular).
- **Producer Rule**: Avoid aimless wandering by sketching a deliberate contour shape first (arch, ascending climb, descending sigh, or terraced steps), mixing mostly stepwise motion with purposeful leaps that give the phrase a focal peak.

### Creating Melodies 2: Using Motives (pp. 182–185)
- A **motive** is the smallest recognizable melodic/rhythmic cell. Long melodies become coherent when built from a small motive developed via:
  - Repetition (exact or rhythmic)
  - Sequence / transposition (shifting pitch level while keeping shape)
  - Inversion, retrograde, augmentation, diminution, and rhythmic displacement (covered in *Note Transformations*, pp. 108–119).
- Pair an opening motive ("question" / antecedent) with an answering variation ("answer" / consequent) so phrases feel self-contained.

### Multiple Simultaneous Melodies (pp. 186–189)
- When combining basslines, countermelodies, and leads so they don't collide into mud:
  - Give each voice its own **register band** (avoid crossing or crowding the same octave).
  - Use **complementary rhythm** (when the lead sustains or rests, the counter-melody or bass moves, and vice versa).
  - Favor **contrary and oblique motion** between outer voices rather than always moving in parallel.

### Linear Rhythm in Melodies: Hocket (pp. 190–194)
- **Hocket** is the melodic equivalent of linear drumming: notes of a single melodic line alternate rapidly between **two or more instruments** so no two instruments play simultaneously.
- Creates rhythmic vitality and timbral interplay from simple source pitches without cluttering vertical harmony.

### Sound-Color Melody: *Klangfarbenmelodie* (pp. 195–198)
- Distribute a single melody—or even a single sustained pitch—across **multiple timbres/instruments** sequentially over time.
- Especially effective at slow tempos where static timbres grow tiresome even when automation is applied.

## 2. Harmonic Craft

### Creating Harmony 1 & 2: From Diatonic Triads to Color Chords (pp. 203–224)
- Establish a tonal home either via **functional harmonic motion** (tension → release around tonic) or via **Repetition and Insistence** (see below).
- Move beyond plain triads using:
  - **Seventh chords** (maj7, min7, dom7, half-dim7, dim7) for warmth, forward pull, or tension.
  - **Extensions (9ths, 11ths, 13ths)** for lush neo-soul, jazz, or ambient voicings.
  - **Suspended chords (`sus2`, `sus4`)** to delay resolution and open harmonic space without committing to major/minor quality.
  - **Added-tone chords (`add9`, `6`)** for bright pop/cinematic color.

### Voice Leading and Inversions (pp. 225–228)
- Think of chord progressions **horizontally** (each note in a chord is an independent voice moving to a note in the next chord), not only vertically.
- Use **inversions** so inner voices move by the smallest possible interval (common tones held, remaining voices step by 1–2 semitones), avoiding choppy hand jumps and creating smooth, professional keyboard/pad/string textures.

### Parallel Harmony (pp. 229–232)
- Used heavily in **deep house, garage, and post-dubstep**: take a single chord voicing (commonly a minor 7th or 9th chord) and **shift every note by the exact same number of semitones in the same direction** (literal transposition / planing).
- Breaks strict common-practice voice-leading rules intentionally to produce a cohesive, hypnotic, sampled-chord-stab aesthetic.

### Harmonic Rhythm (pp. 233–235)
- **Harmonic rhythm** is the rate at which chords change (e.g., once every 2 bars, once per bar, twice per bar).
- Changing harmonic rhythm across sections is a powerful arrangement lever: slow harmonic rhythm in intros/verses creates hypnotic stability; accelerating harmonic rhythm in pre-choruses or cadences drives momentum into the chorus or drop.

### Repetition and Insistence (pp. 236–237)
- A brief, angular, or even non-diatonic musical pattern will start to feel coherent and "right" to the listener if **repeated persistently** enough ("repetition legitimizes").
- Useful in electronic/minimal/riff-based tracks where groove and hypnotic pattern identity supersede traditional functional cadences.
