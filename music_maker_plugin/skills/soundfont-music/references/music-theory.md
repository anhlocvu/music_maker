# Music theory for writing songs

Practical theory for turning a brief into notes. Note numbers: C4 = 60.

## Harmony and function
Chords fall into three functions:
- **Tonic (T)** - rest, home: I, iii, vi (in minor: i, bIII, bVI).
- **Predominant / subdominant (S)** - moves away, mild forward pull: ii, IV (minor: ii°, iv, bVI).
- **Dominant (D)** - tension that wants to resolve to tonic: V, V7, vii°.
Basic motion is T -> S -> D -> T. The simplest complete phrase is I-V-I; add a
predominant for I-IV-V-I or I-ii-V-I. Predominants lead to the dominant, the dominant
leads home. Use this to plan phrases: end phrases on V (half cadence - "to be continued")
or V-I (authentic cadence - "done").

## Voice leading
- Move each inner voice the shortest way to the next chord; keep common tones.
- Upper voices move mostly by step; the bass leaps more (roots, fifths).
- Avoid melodic augmented/diminished leaps and leaps bigger than a fifth in inner voices.
- The leading tone (7th degree, e.g. B in C, D# in E minor) resolves up by step.
- Avoid parallel fifths/octaves between two voices when you want a classical sound
  (in rock/game music, parallel power chords are the point).

| "Just the Two of Us" move | IVmaj7-III7-vi7 | |

Colour tools: secondary dominants (V/V = D7 in C), borrowed chords from the parallel
mode (iv, bVI, bVII in major), tritone substitution (bII7 for V7), diminished passing
chords, a pedal point (hold the bass on I or V while chords change above), a **line
cliché** (inner voice falling by half steps over one chord: Am, AmMaj7, Am7, Am6).

## Modes (brightest -> darkest)
| mode | formula vs major | character |
|---|---|---|
| Lydian | #4 | floating, dreamy, wonder - film "magic" |
| Ionian (major) | - | happy, stable |
| Mixolydian | b7 | bluesy major, relaxed, adventurous; rock |
| Dorian | b3 b7 | minor but hopeful, smooth; jazz, funk, folk |
| Aeolian (natural minor) | b3 b6 b7 | sad, serious |
| Phrygian | b2 b3 b6 b7 | dark, exotic, aggressive; flamenco, metal |
| Locrian | b2 b3 b5 b6 b7 | unstable, rarely a home key |
Also: **harmonic minor** (natural minor with raised 7th - dramatic, neoclassical, a
strong V chord), **melodic minor**, **pentatonic** (5 notes, no clashes, folk/East Asian
colour), Japanese **in / miyako-bushi** (1 b2 4 5 b6) for a traditional flavour.
Write a mode's colour note (Lydian #4, Dorian 6, Mixolydian b7) prominently or it just
sounds like plain major/minor.

## Melody and motifs
- Build from a **motif** (2-8 notes with a clear rhythm), then develop it:
  - **repetition** (exact, for memorability),
  - **sequence** (the same shape starting a step higher/lower, 2-3 times in a row),
  - **variation** (keep the rhythm, change intervals; or keep the contour, change rhythm),
  - **extension** (repeat, then add a tail that makes the ending stronger),
  - **inversion / augmentation / fragmentation** for development sections.
- Balance repetition (memorable) with variation (not boring): a common phrase plan is
  a a' b a'' over 4 or 8 bars, or question (ends on V) and answer (ends on I).
- **Contour**: give each phrase a shape and one high point; follow a leap with a step
  back the other way.
- Land on chord tones on strong beats; use passing and neighbour tones (including
  chromatic ones) between them.
- Leave space: a lead that never rests is tiring; let a counter-melody answer in the gaps.

## Rhythm
- **Syncopation** - accents off the beat: anticipate chord changes by an 8th or 16th and
  tie over the bar line; use a 3+3+2 grouping of 8ths or 16ths for drive.
- **Swing** delays off-beat 8ths (`Song(swing=0.3)`).
- Tempo sets energy: ballads 60-80, mid 90-120, action 140-190 BPM.
- Change density between sections (half-time breakdowns, double-time climaxes).

## Orchestration and texture
- Each instrument has a sweet register: flute and violin brighten as they rise; cello,
  bassoon and tuba are richest low. Keep parts inside comfortable ranges.
- **Register layout**: bass low (octaves 1-3), harmony in the middle (3-5), melody on top
  (4-6). Spread low chords wide (roots, fifths); cluster only in the middle and top.
- **Doubling** adds weight: melody in octaves, or woodwinds an octave under violins.
- **Texture**: homophonic (everyone in the same rhythm) vs polyphonic (independent
  lines). Switching texture is one of the strongest ways to vary a repeat.
- **Timbre as meaning**: bright/plucked for playful, sustained strings for emotion,
  brass for heroic, dry release-cut stabs for tight rhythmic energy.
- Pan instruments apart and give each its own register so the mix stays clear.

## Form
- Song forms: verse-chorus, AABA (32 bars), ABAC; game loops: intro + A B (C) looping.
- Contrast between sections comes from key or mode, register, texture, density, or
  tempo feel. Return to the A material to feel "home".
- Transitions: drum fills, risers, a chromatic run, or a dominant chord at the
  section's end.
