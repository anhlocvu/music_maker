# Creative Producer Strategies: Problems of Finishing (Dennis DeSantis, *Making Music*)

*Distilled faithfully from Dennis DeSantis, "Making Music: 74 Creative Strategies for Electronic Music Producers" (Part 3: Problems of Finishing, pp. 258–293).*

| # | Strategy | Problem Pattern | Core Producer Solution |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Arranging as a Subtractive Process** | Blank timeline intimidation | Rapidly fill an entire song-length timeline (20 seconds, no overthinking) with full loops across every track; then **sculpt by deleting/muting** sections and thinning layers backwards/forwards. |
| 2 | **The Power of Erasing** | Cluttered mix feels "small" or weak | Fullness comes from space, not piling on tracks. **Delete redundant parts**, thin note counts, or shorten sustain/decay tails (`release` cut) so essential elements command attention. |
| 3 | **Formal Skeletons** | Arrangement proportions feel off | Load a great reference track, match its BPM grid, mark every formal division and bar count (`Intro`, `Verse`, `Breakdown`, `Drop`, etc.), delete the reference audio, and arrange your own material into that proven structural map (adapting bar lengths whenever your material demands it). |
| 4 | **Common Forms 1: Elements of Song Form** | Unsure how Verse/Chorus/Bridge work | **Verse (A)**: recurring narrative body (often 16 or 32 bars, builds tension). **Chorus (B)**: contrasting hook/resolution, shares core motif/energy. **Bridge (C)**: single contrasting departure (new harmony/key/density/solo). Standard pop template: `ABABCB`. |
| 5 | **Common Forms 2: Layering as Form** | Instrumental/electronic music lacks Verse/Chorus | Build structure through **textural density cycles** around core material: **Buildup** (16/32 bars adding layers & tension), **Breakdown** (8/16 bars stripping drums or everything except drums for space), **Drop** (climax of peak density, typically 1–2 per track). |
| 6 | **Short Loops as Endings** | Arrangement works but won't resolve | Isolate a 1- or 2-bar snippet of the closing phrase and repeat it (optionally halving again to 1 bar / 2 beats) for an even count (`4, 8, 16`) to create accelerating drive into a clean stop. |
| 7 | **Unique Events** | Track sounds like repetitive "loop music" | Inject **one-time, never-repeated events**: (a) single unusual sample/hit (at a boundary or deliberately off-grid), (b) single one-off melodic/MIDI gesture variation, or (c) single temporary FX move. Use sparingly so surprise stays surprising. |
| 8 | **Three Ways to End** | Unsure how to write the outro | (1) **Mirror accumulation** (strip layers out in reverse order of how the intro built them); (2) **Loop and add/subtract** (build intensity to a hard stop, or thin down to a whisper); (3) **Fade out** (only on smooth, infinitely loopable closing grooves—apply fade post-mastering if mastering compression is used). |
| 9 | **Rendering as Commitment** | Endless patch/note tweaking prevents finishing | **Commit/bounce to audio** (or freeze final tracks/stems) early to lock sound-design doors and force focus onto arrangement, balance, and completion. |
| 10 | **Getting Feedback** | Unsure who/when/how to ask | Ask knowledgeable peers/producers (not praise-seeking friends/family); match rough loudness to finished references so listeners aren't fooled by low volume; ask **specific** questions about form, groove, and sounds rather than "do you like it?" |
| 11 | **Diminishing Returns** | Late-stage perfectionism stalls completion | Recognize when edits become arbitrary rather than clear improvements. Initial instincts are often right; learn to stop at "good enough to ship" instead of over-tweaking backward. |
| 12 | **Fail Better** | Late realization that a track isn't great | Finish the arrangement anyway (skip expensive mastering/release and archive useful pieces into *Scraps and Sketches*). **Finishing is a skill that requires practice**—finishing imperfect tracks trains you to finish great ones. |
