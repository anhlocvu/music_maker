# Critical Listening, Perceived Space & Musical Structure (PWYM Curriculum)

*Distilled faithfully from NYU Music Experience Design Lab's "Play With Your Music" Curriculum (Modules 1–3: Alex Ruthmann, Ethan Hein, Alex Case, Phil Galdston, Kevin Killen).*

## 1. Critical Listening Discipline (Module 1 & 2)
- **90% of a producer's or engineer's work is critical listening**: isolating sound sources, characterizing timbres, and tracking how they move through perceived space and musical time.
- **Playback Consistency**: Listen in a consistent environment on full-range monitors or accurate headphones rather than laptop speakers or earbuds that mask bass/sub-bass and micro-details. Also compare across multiple systems (studio headphones vs. small speakers) to expose translation flaws.

## 2. Perceived Space Graph (Module 2, Moylan / Hein)
Map every instrument/layer across **three spatial axes** plus time:
1. **Left–Right (Panning / Stereo Position)**: Exact placement vs. wide stereo spread (`narrow` pinpoint vs. `wide` enveloping).
2. **Front–Back (Depth / Distance)**: Close/dry foreground vs. middle distance vs. distant/reverberant background.
3. **Top–Bottom (Frequency / Pitch Register)**: Low end foundation vs. mid-band body vs. high-frequency air/brilliance.
4. **Dynamic Motion**: Track whether any element shifts spatial position across sections.

## 3. Musical Structure Graph (Module 3)
To reverse-engineer or plan a song's architecture:
1. **Map Sections & Bar Counts**: Diagram `Intro`, `Verse`, `Pre-Chorus`, `Chorus`, `Bridge`, `Solo`, `Outro` with exact measure lengths and BPM/meter (and clarify half-time vs. cut-time vs. normal counting).
2. **Track Layer Entrances & Exits**: Chart every instrument's timeline row by row so arrangement density, contrast, and build-up trajectories are visible at a glance.
3. **Annotate Chord Changes & Hook Anchor**: Pair the structural map with a concise chord/lead-sheet chart and identify when core motifs/hooks lock in.
4. **Demo-First Discipline**: Even major studio productions (e.g., Peter Gabriel's *Sledgehammer*) often begin from a bare rhythmic click/drum-machine pulse + single piano/chord skeleton before layering ornaments and final timbres.
