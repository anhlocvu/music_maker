# Creative Producer Strategies: Problems of Beginning (Dennis DeSantis, *Making Music*)

*Distilled faithfully from Dennis DeSantis, "Making Music: 74 Creative Strategies for Electronic Music Producers" (Part 1: Problems of Beginning, pp. 12–90).*

| # | Strategy | Problem Pattern | Core Producer Solution |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Three Ways to Start** | Blank-slate paralysis | (1) Start from the **foundation** (drums + bass) and build upward in pitch; (2) start from the **hook/center** (melody/chord core) and wrap accompaniment around it; or (3) start from a **non-musical texture/atmosphere** (drone, field recording) and carve rhythm/pitch out of it. |
| 2 | **Catalog of Attributes** | Inspiration vs. copying | Study an inspiring reference track deeply; write a text **catalog of its attributes** (tempo, texture, groove, register, form, timbre); then **put away the reference audio** and compose strictly from your own written catalog. |
| 3 | **Avoidance List** | Self-repetition / creative rut | Diagnose exact recurring habits across sound, harmony, melody, rhythm, and form; write an explicit **Avoidance List** (with positive alternatives—e.g., "avoid filter sweeps → try FM timbre motion") before starting. |
| 4 | **Active Listening** | Passive consumption | Treat listening as primary work (no multitasking): track how sections enter/exit, how dynamics shift, and what each layer contributes. |
| 5 | **Listening to Music You Hate** | Narrow stylistic vocabulary | Study genres outside your comfort zone analytically—ask *why* its audience connects with its rhythm, timbre, or structure—and import one foreign mechanism into your own style. |
| 6 | **Mise en Place** | Setup friction killing flow | Prepare templates, `.sf2` font paths, presets, and `midi/` analysis *before* creative sessions so technical hiccups never derail inspiration. |
| 7 | **Arbitrary Constraints** | Option paralysis | Impose a **narrow artificial frame** before starting (e.g., 4 tracks max, only one SoundFont, 85 BPM, strictly Dorian mode, 24-bar limit) to force fast decisions. |
| 8 | **"Write Drunk; Edit Sober"** | Inner critic killing flow | Strictly separate **creation** (move forward, delete nothing, capture ideas quickly) from **editing** (cull, refine, voice-lead, polish later with cold objectivity). |
| 9 | **Choosing a Tempo** | Uncertainty over exact BPM | Pick the middle of the genre range (Dub 60–90, Hip-hop 60–100, Downtempo 90–120, House 115–130, Techno/Trance 120–140, Dubstep 135–145, DnB 160–180), test feel in context, then commit without agonizing over 1 BPM. |
| 10 | **One Part at a Time** | Overwhelmed by whole-song scope | Forget the finished track initially; write **one complete, compelling part** first, then add the next part that reacts to it. |
| 11 | **Different Tools** | Predictable tool-driven habits | Break muscle-memory patterns by swapping tools (different SoundFont palette, tracker/Python script vs. piano roll, built-in synth vs. acoustic samples). |
| 12 | **Presets as Starting Points** | Guilt vs. slow sound design | Use presets/stock kits as **raw clay**, then customize envelope, release, layering, detune, or velocity response so they fit your track rather than building every sound from zero. |
| 13 | **The Tyranny of the Default** | Blank DAW template friction | Replace empty defaults with a personal starting template containing pre-routed buses, starter drum/bass slots, and favorite go-to instruments. |
| 14 | **Simple Sounds** | Sound-hunting erases musical ideas | Draft notes, chords, and rhythm immediately using **plain General MIDI or basic synth patches**; replace with final timbres only after the musical idea works. |
| 15 | **Extended Techniques** | Bored with standard samples/synths | Incorporate non-traditional sources (found percussion, field noise, extreme register/articulation, pitched noise) to bridge acoustic grit and electronic control. |
| 16 | **Goal-less Exploration** | Sitting at studio uninspired | Run low-pressure sound-design or rhythm experiments with **zero expectation of writing a song**; harvest interesting scraps later. |
| 17 | **Procrastination & Timeboxing** | Task aversion | Set short, non-negotiable **timeboxes** (e.g., 25 minutes) focused on one micro-deliverable rather than waiting for inspiration. |
| 18 | **Collaboration** | Unclear partner roles | Choose collaborators who complement your gaps and elevate output (not just close friends); define clear division of creative ownership. |
| 19 | **Thinking Like an Amateur** | Seriousness kills joy | Reclaim playful curiosity and fearless experimentation by temporarily dropping commercial/professional pressure. |
| 20 | **Scraps and Sketches** | Guilt over unfinished folders | Treat abandoned projects as a **parts yard**—harvest strong 4-bar basslines, drum fills, or chord voicings into new tracks. |
| 21 | **Problem-Solving as Distraction** | Fixing gear instead of making music | Recognize studio "Resistance" (tinkering with configs/plugins); ignore non-blocking setup issues until the music draft exists. |
| 22 | **Personal Workflow** | Tutorial dogma anxiety | There is no single "correct" order (sound design → compose → arrange → mix). Test workflows, keep what finishes tracks, discard the rest. |
| 23 | **Originality vs. Quality** | Anxiety over sounding derivative | Prioritize **quality and craft** over forced novelty; authentic voice emerges naturally from refining execution across many finished tracks. |
| 24 | **Process vs. Product** | Obsessing over clever systems | Listeners hear only the finished audio, not how complex or pure your generative process was. Discard "clever" rules whenever the result sounds lifeless. |
| 25 | **On Work** | Waiting for effortless flow | Realize flow is rare; consistent craft is built through deliberate, daily work even when notes feel hard-won. |
