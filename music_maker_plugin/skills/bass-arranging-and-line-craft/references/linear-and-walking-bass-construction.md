# Linear & Walking Bass Line Construction

> Grounded in Dr. J.B. Dyas (*Linear Bass Line Construction* Parts 1–2, DownBeat Masterclass), P.W. Farrell (*Walking Bass Fundamentals* Parts 1–3), MrBlancq (*Walking Bass Lines*), Matthew Clauhs (*Arranging for School Jazz Ensembles*), Rubim de Toledo (*African Rhythm as the Foundation of Contemporary Bass Performance*, Univ. of Auckland, 2018), Jim Stinnett (*Berklee Online Bass Handbook*), and Midwest Clinic rhythm-section guidelines.

## 1. Dual Foundation Role, Combo Preparation & Time Feel

1. **Dual Responsibility (*Dyas; Rufus Reid; Cecil McBee; Hove et al. PNAS*)**:
   - The lowest-pitched voice outlines rhythm (temporal pulse) AND harmony simultaneously—lower pitches deliver measurably sharper temporal perception for the entire band.
   - Unless playing an explicit rubato passage, maintain an **unwavering inner pulse** (*Zimmerli*)—even when syncopating or laying subtly forward/back against drums, the underlying grid never wavers.
2. **Dyas's 5-Step Combo Preparation Hierarchy**:
   1. **Sound**: Warm, fat, focused—never boomy or muddy; dig in clearly.
   2. **Intonation**: Critical in upright and fretless low-mid registers.
   3. **Keep Form**: Memorize structure before entering—standard `12-bar blues`, `16-bar`, `32-bar AABA` / `ABAC`, plus irregular forms (*Alone Together* `14+14+8+8`, *Moment's Notice* `8+8+8+6+8`, *Song for My Father* `24-bar AAB`, *Peace* `10-bar`, *Stablemates* `14+8+14`).
   4. **Style & Feel**:
      - **Standard Swing / Ballad**: Often **2-feel** on Chorus 1 (half-notes on roots/5ths), shifting to **4-feel walking** on solo choruses, optionally returning to **2-feel** on the out-head; ballads invite deeper melodic space and soloist interaction.
      - **Bebop**: Driving continuous **4-feel quarter-note walking**.
      - **Jazz Waltz (`3/4`)**: Often **1-feel** (dotted half) Chorus 1 → **3-feel walking** or **broken 3** alternating between both.
      - **Bossa Nova**: Straight dotted-quarter + eighth pulse (no swing).
      - **Rock / Funk**: Straight eighths (rock) vs syncopated 16ths with grounded downbeats (funk).
   5. **Right Notes**: Define root movement and exact `3rds`, `7ths`, and altered extensions cleanly.
3. **Articulation, Note Length & Swing Edge (*Midwest Clinic; Stinnett*)**:
   - **Quarter-Note Sustain**: Play walking quarters **`2/3` of the beat legato** in slow/medium swing for big bouncing drive; leave crisp micro-separation at fast tempos so pitches never blur.
   - **Swing Edge & Drum Lock**: Sit slightly on the forward edge of the beat (Ray Brown model) or subtly lock every attack to the **ride cymbal skip and feathered bass drum**.
   - **Upright Tone on Electric Bass (*Stinnett*)**: Pluck closer to the **end of the fingerboard (20th–24th fret area)** with higher action/firm dig-in, rolling off shrill highs and bloated sub-boom for dark wooden attack and quick decay.

## 2. Ithaca 4-Step Progressive Walking Blueprint (*Clauhs*)

Build clean, idiomatic straight-ahead lines incrementally in 4 structural passes:

1. **Step 1 (Beat `1` & `3` Skeleton)**: Anchor **Roots on beat `1` and beat `3`** (especially on 2-chords-per-bar changes).
2. **Step 2 (Chordal Anchor)**: Place **Roots on `1`**, **5ths (or 3rds) on beats `2–3`**, locking triad/7th stability.
3. **Step 3 (Scale & Guide-Tone Color)**: Insert **`3rds`, `7ths`, `9ths`, and scalewise tones** on weak beats `2` and `4`.
4. **Step 4 (Half/Whole-Step Target Approach)**: Replace beat `4` (or beat `2` before fast mid-bar changes) with a **diatonic or chromatic approach note** a half-step or whole-step above/below the upcoming root.

## 3. Cycle Patterns (`Up a 4th / Down a 5th`) (*Dyas Part 1; MrBlancq*)

Across the dominant workhorse progression of jazz (`II–V–I` and `I–VI–II–V`), mix and match **three 1-bar Cycle Patterns** (`3 × 3 = 9` `II–V–I` shapes, `27` over 4-bar turnarounds):

### A. Major `II–V–I` Cycle Patterns (Roots up a `P4`)
1. **Pattern 1 — Scale Descent**: `1 - 7 - 6 - 5` *(use **`b7`** on `m7` and `Dom7`; **`maj7`** on `maj7`)* → steps smoothly by 2nd/m2 into the next root or `3rd`.
2. **Pattern 2 — Arpeggio + Chromatic Approach**: `1 - 3 - 5 - ch` *(use **`m3`** on `m7`; **`M3`** on `Dom7` and `maj7`)* → beat 4 sits one chromatic half-step away from next chord's root (frequently `b5`/`#4` or scalar half-step).
3. **Pattern 3 — Rising Chromatic Drive**: **`Whole – Half – Half – Half`** *(four ascending steps rising `1 → 2 → b3 → 3` into next root on `P4`)*.

### B. Minor `IIø–V7alt–i` Altered Cycle Patterns
When walking minor cadences (`IIø → V7alt → i`), substitute these three altered-scale cells (`9` total combinations):
1. **Altered Pattern 1 — Altered Descent**: `1 - b7 - #5 - b5` (traces Phrygian/Locrian/Altered tensions smoothly down).
2. **Altered Pattern 2 — Altered Leap + `b5` Chromatic**: `1 - b2 - 3 - b5` *(use **`m3`** for `IIø`, **`M3`** for `V7alt`)* → `b5` steps half-step up into next root.
3. **Altered Pattern 3 — Altered Ascent**: **`Half – Whole – Half – Half`** *(ascending `1 → b2 → b3 → 3` into `P4` root)*.

### C. Tritone & Stationary-Root Formulas (*MrBlancq*)
- **Tritone Root Motion (`b5` apart, e.g., `F#7 → C` or `Eb∆ → Aø`)**:
  - Down scale: `1 - 7 - 6 - 5` (`F#-E-D#-C#`, landing a half-step above `C`).
  - Up scale: `1 - 2 - 3 - 4` (`F#-G#-A#-B`, landing a half-step below `C`).
- **Same Root Held Across Bars (`Unison / Octave`)**:
  - Down arpeggio: `1 - 7 - 5 - 3` (or repeat `1 → 5` on alternate downbeats — *Farrell*).
  - Up arpeggio: `1 - 3 - 5 - 7`.

## 4. Scalar & Harmonic Connection Rules (*Farrell Parts 1–3*)

1. **Strong Beats vs Weak Beats (`4/4`)**:
   - **Beats `1 & 3`** carry harmonic clarity—place **Roots, `3rds`, `5ths`, `7ths` (or consonant `6ths`)** here.
   - **Beats `2 & 4`** act as rhythmic bridges—non-chord passing tones and outside chromatics work freely on `2` and `4` without breaking harmony.
2. **Static Multi-Bar Chords**: Avoid jumping back to the root on every single downbeat during 4–8 bar vamps; alternate **`Root` and `5th` on downbeats** every second measure to keep line contour flowing.
3. **Resolving the Top of the Scale (`7th → Root`)**:
   - **Major Ionian (`maj7`) Ascending**: Insert a **turn** (skip up a 3rd from `7th` `B → D` on beat 4) so the line drops cleanly onto `C` on beat 1.
   - **Dorian (`m7`) & Mixolydian (`Dom7`) Ascending**: Because `b7` sits a whole step below `8`, insert a **chromatic passing tone (`♮7`) on beat 4** sliding directly up into `Root`.
   - **Descending from Root on `M6/13` Chords**: Any chord scale containing a major `6/13` accepts a **chromatic passing tone descending into the `5th`** (`1 → 7 → b7 → 6` or `8 → 7 → b7 → 5`).
4. **Minor Mode Colour on Descent (`Aeolian`, `Locrian`, `Phrygian Dominant`)**:
   - To preserve characteristic **`b13` (Aeolian)**, **`b5` (Locrian `iiø`)**, and **`b9` (Phrygian Dominant `V7b9`)** while descending without landing off-grid on strong beats, place a **chromatic passing tone on beat `2`** (`weak beat`).
   - Treat minor `V7` chords as **`V7b9`** by default (leaping to or threading `b9`) unless the chart explicitly dictates natural `9th`.

## 5. Linear Chromaticism, Rufus Reid Devices & Fast Changes (*Dyas Part 2; Farrell Part 3*)

1. **Non-Root Linear Targets**:
   - Rather than bouncing `Root → Root` on every fast change (*Sister Sadie* chromatic dominants `C7–Bb7–A7–Ab7`, *Giant Steps* 2 chords/bar), aim stepwise/chromatically to the **closest chord tone (`R, 3, 5, 7`)** of the next bar.
   - **Crucial Form Rule (*Dyas Part 2*)**: Always attack the true **Root on Beat 1 of crucial structural bars** (start of A, B, bridge, final turnaround) even when threading non-root targets mid-phrase.
   - **Contrary Motion Hipness**: Ascending chromatically while chord roots descend (or vice versa) produces classic post-bop momentum.
2. **No "Stutter" Repeats — Use Enclosures (*Dyas Part 2*)**:
   - Never play the exact same note twice on consecutive beats to burn an extra beat during linear fills ("stuttering" kills forward momentum).
   - If you reach your target note 1–2 beats early, insert an **enclosure** (`half-step above & half-step below` surrounding the target) or extend a clean one-direction chromatic run.
3. **Rufus Reid Connection Toolkit (*Come Rain or Come Shine* Analysis, *Farrell Part 3*)**:
   - Prioritize **chromatic half-step connections** (most frequent), backed by **descending 5th leaps** and **whole-step approaches** to avoid overusing purely chromatic runs.
   - **2-Beat `ii → V` Contrapuntal Line**: Walk downward from **`ii` Root → `V` 3rd** (moving down by step against the ascending-4th root progression).
   - **2-Beat `ii → V` Tritone Leap**: Leap from root to tritone (`#4/b5`), resolving immediately by half-step into the next target.
   - **Motivic Paraphrase & Octave Lift**: Repeat/develop short contour shapes across 2–4 bar groups, and jump a full phrase **up an octave** at fast-tempo soloist transitions or high choruses for controlled dynamic lift.
