# Afro-Cuban & Latin Drumset Coordination

Translate traditional Afro-Cuban and Cuban dance ensembles onto the drumset using authentic clave orientation, four-limb independence, and genre-specific timbres (synthesized from Mauro Battisti's *Drumgenius 3.0 Afro-Cuban* workbook & Ignacio Berroa / Tito Puente / Manny Oquendo / Eddie Palmieri / Giovanni Hidalgo principles).

## 1. Fundamental Clave & Melody Rules

- **Clave Selection Rule (Ignacio Berroa)**: Never pick `2-3` vs `3-2` clave arbitrarily. **Let the melody decide**: clap both `2-3` and `3-2` claves alongside the tune's main melody/head and select whichever side resolves naturally with its phrase accents.
- **Ensemble Unification**: Lock **drumset, bass (`tumbao`), piano `montuno`, and percussion** strictly to the **same clave direction (`2-3` or `3-2`)**—contradictory `2-3` vs `3-2` alignment destabilizes the pocket.
- **Son Clave vs. Rumba Clave**:
  - **Son Clave (`3-2` / `2-3`)**: `3`-side strikes beats `1`, `and-of-2`, `4` (`[0, 3, 6]` in 8th-note steps, or `[0, 6, 12]` in a 16-step bar); `2`-side strikes beats `2` and `3`.
  - **Rumba Clave (`3-2` / `2-3`)**: Delays the 3rd hit of the `3`-side by one subdivision (`and-of-4` instead of `4`), adding rolling African-rooted pull into the `2`-side.
- **"Salsa" Terminology Distinction (Tito Puente & Eddie Palmieri)**: Treat Afro-Cuban styles by their true rhythmic lineage—**Mambo, Cha-cha, Guaguancó, Son Montuno, Mozambique, Songo, Danzón, Bolero, Bembé**—rather than flattening all Latin grooves under one generic beat.

## 2. Four-Limb Drumset Role Mapping & Articulations

Traditional Afro-Cuban rhythm sections split conga, timbale, bongo, clave, and bell players across **two hands + two feet** on drumset:

- **Left Foot**: Anchors **`Clave` pedal** (`37 SideStick` / `75 Claves`) or steady **`Pedal Hi-Hat` (`44`)** splash on pulse beats (`Danzón`, `Afro 6/8`).
- **Right Foot (`Kick / Bombo`)**: Avoid rigid rock Beat-1/Beat-3 thuds; play syncopated **tumbao bass anticipations (`and-of-2` and `4`)** interlocking cleanly with upright/electric bass (`Danzón`, `Mambo`, `Songo`, `Son Montuno`).
- **Right Hand (Time & Bell Lead)**: Drives **`Cáscara`** (played on **Floor Tom Shell** or rim), **`Cowbell` (`56`)** (alternating **Large / Small** bells or bell mouth vs shank), **Ride**, or **Jam Block**.
- **Left Hand (Snare & Tom Dialogue)**: Replies to the bell/clave line with **`Snare Off`** tom-like pitches, **`Mute snare with left hand fingertips`** (`Danzón`, `Guiro`), **`Soft Rim-Shot`**, **`Buzz Roll`**, or cross-kit **high/floor tom** imitations of conga open/slap tones (`Tumba / Quinto`).

## 3. Verified Genre Archetypes & Timbre Spec

Choose authentic meter, implements, and kit articulations per Cuban style family:

| Style Family | Meter | Signature Coordination & Kit Timbre |
|---|---|---|
| **`Afro 6/8` (Sticks & Brushes)** | `6/8` (`12/8`) | Bell `6/8` timeline (`long-long-short-long-long-long-short`) leads right hand; **Snare Off**; kick/toms emulate conga/batá counterpoint (`Afro 6/8 1–8`, **brushes** variant on #8) |
| **`Abaquà` & `Bembé`** | `6/8` | Dense interlocking folkloric `6/8` bell + left/right tom dialogue (`Abaquà`, `Bembé 1–2`) |
| **`Guiro`** | `6/8` | **Snare Off** + **left-hand fingertip snare muting** mimicking subtle gourd/chekeré scrape texture alongside bell |
| **`Cáscara + Clave` (`2-3` Son / `3-2` Rumba)** | `4/4` | Right hand drives syncopated `cáscara` shell rhythm while left foot/rim outlines explicit `2-3 Son` or `3-2 Rumba` clave |
| **`Mambo`** | `4/4` | High-energy bell (`RideBell` / `Cowbell`) + **Floor Tom Shell** (`Mambo 3a`) with punchy tumbao kick and bright snare/tom accents (`Mambo 1–3a`) |
| **`Son Montuno`** | `4/4` | Driving cowbell campana groove (`Son Montuno 4–5`) lifting into horn/piano montuno choruses |
| **`Cha-cha`** | `4/4` | Crisp quarter-beat / light-eighth bell/hat groove (`Cha-cha 1–3`) featuring **Soft Rim-Shot** and **Buzz Roll** backbeat embellishments |
| **`Mozambique`** | `4/4` | High-density New York Mambo/Oquendo/Palmieri fusion (`Mozambique 1–9`): multi-bell/cowbell patterns + interlocking syncopated kicks and sharp tom/snare rolls |
| **`Songo`** | `4/4` | Linear Changuito-inspired conversation (`Songo 1–7`) blending kick, ghosted/accented snare, **Jam Block** (`Songo 2`), and **Floor Tom Shell** (`Songo 5`)—avoid simultaneous cluttered limbs |
| **`Timba`** | `4/4` | Modern Cuban drive combining **`Large & Small Cowbells`** with aggressive kick/snare/tom phrasing |
| **`Rumba Guaguancó` & `Conga Callejera / de Comparsa`** | `4/4` | Folkloric street & rumba vocabulary (`Rumba Guaguancó 2–3`, `Pilon 2`, `Pachanga`) utilizing **Buzz Roll** swells and tom-emulated quinto improvisations |
| **`Ballroom Rumba` & `Bolero`** | `4/4` | Intimate **brushes** sweeps (`Ballroom Rumba 1–2`, `Bolero 1–3`) supporting soft lyrical ballads (keep commercial *Ballroom Rhumba* distinct from folkloric Cuban *Rumba*) |
| **`Danzón` & `Guajira`** | `4/4` | **`Danzón 1–2`**: **Snare Off**, **Clave with left foot**, **Large/Small Cowbells**, and **Mute snare with left hand fingertips**; `Guajira 1–2`: relaxed country-son groove |
