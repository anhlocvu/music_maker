# Classical Masters Orchestration (Piston, Rimsky-Korsakov, Berlioz/Strauss, Forsyth, Prout & Widor)

## 1. Seven Fundamental Orchestral Texture Types (Walter Piston)

Analyze and build every orchestral score bar-by-bar from one of **7 textural blueprints** before assigning individual players:

| Type | Name | Musical Fabric & Orchestration Principle |
|---|---|---|
| **Type I** | **Orchestral Unison** | All melodic instruments sound identical pitch classes in 1 or 2+ octaves (match each section to its optimal resonance register rather than forcing an awkward single octave) |
| **Type II** | **Melody + Accompaniment** | Two clear strata: foreground melody (dominant timbre/octave weight) vs. harmonized rhythmic/arpeggiated accompaniment + foundational bass |
| **Type III** | **Secondary Melody** | Primary melody + distinct countermelody (timbrally contrasting, e.g., cello + horn against violin theme; avoid masking both lines in the same mid-register) |
| **Type IV** | **Part Writing (Homophony)** | Choir-like vertical chords where every voice has smooth linear motion without separating into melody vs. pulsed figuration |
| **Type V** | **Contrapuntal Texture** | Independent overlapping melodic subjects/fugato lines separated maximally by timbre + register so every entry remains audible |
| **Type VI** | **Isolated Chords** | Sustained, punctuated, or antiphonal chord blocks balanced carefully for vertical dynamic/harmonic flatness across families |
| **Type VII** | **Complex Texture ("Tapestry")** | Superimposed independent layers combining multiple Types simultaneously (e.g., Type II strings + ostinato harp + brass fanfare hits + contrapuntal winds) |

## 2. Chord Distribution, Spacing & Balance (Piston & Rimsky-Korsakov)

### Three Vertical Woodwind / Section Layouts (Rimsky-Korsakov)
1. **Overlaying (Juxtaposition)**: Voices strictly obey normal pitch-order hierarchy from top down (**Flutes -> Oboes -> Clarinets -> Bassoons**). Safest, clearest default blend.
2. **Crossing**: Adjacent instrument pairs invert order intentionally (e.g., clarinet above oboe) to smooth over a weak oboe low note (`Bb3–C4`) or mellow piercing treble registers.
3. **Enclosure**: Outer voices played by one timbre enclosing inner chord tones on another timbre (e.g., Flutes outer + Oboes inner, or Horns enclosing Bassoons) for unified fusion.

### Numerical Balance & Acoustic Clarity (Piston)
- Equal instrument counts do **NOT** yield equal loudness: high brass (`Trumpet/Trombone` above staff) easily drowns low flutes (`C4–F4`) and single reeds. Pair soft-register woodwinds with gentle middle-low brass, or double treble winds in unison/octaves to compete against `f`/`ff` brass.
- **One String Section = One Wind Unit**: Count an entire desked violin I/II/viola/cello section as **one tonal weight unit**; remember dividing `div. a 2` or `a 3` cuts orchestral weight in half, requiring extra dynamic boost or softer woodwind pairing.
- **Avoid Low Overtone Conflict**: Keep wide intervals (octaves, 5ths, 10ths) in the bottom octaves (`C1–C3`); pack 3rds, 2nds, and extensions strictly in upper-middle registers (`C4+`). Leave clean registral spacing when mixing stationary held harmony with moving filigree (`one tone quality = stationary, distinct quality = moving`).
- **Four-String Double Bass Check**: Double bass sounds 1 octave lower than written (`down to E1`); notes below `E1` require 5-string / C-extension or octave-up transposition (Piston Ch. 21).

## 3. String Bowing Physics & Idiomatic Effects (Piston, Forsyth, Widor)

- **Bowing Direction Dynamics**: **Up-bow (`v`)** naturally intensifies toward crescendo (increasing lever arm toward frog); **Down-bow (`⊓`)** naturally tapers diminuendo and delivers maximal crisp weight at the frog (*au talon*) for sharp downbeat chords. Match long vs. short bowings symmetrically so weak beats do not surge accidentally louder than downbeats!
- **Essential Articulations**:
  - *Détaché* (separate smooth stroke per note, middle/upper third) vs. *Louré* / *Portato* (soft pulse under one slur).
  - *Spiccato* / *Saltando* (bouncing middle bow, `p–mf`) vs. *Jeté* (thrown bouncing groups near point, 2–6 notes) vs. *Martellato* (`martelé`, crisp hammered on-the-string stop).
  - *Sur la touche / Sul tasto* (over fingerboard: soft veiled flute-like tone; impractical on top violin E-string geometry) vs. *Sul ponticello* (at the bridge: glassy, metallic upper partials, devastating in *tremolo*) vs. *Col legno* (`battuto` dry xylophone tap with stick wood; `tratto` eerie drawn whisper).
- **Natural & Artificial Harmonics**: Touching string node `1/2, 1/3, 1/4, 1/5` on open strings produces pure octave, 12th, double-octave, 17th partials; **Artificial Harmonics** stop fundamental with finger 1 and lightly touch `P4` above with finger 4 -> sounds **two octaves above the stopped note**. Cold, transparent in `p`; penetratingly crystalline in `f` (Rimsky-Korsakov).
- **Divisi vs. Double-Stops**: Mark `div.` (split per desk, odd desk upper / even lower) vs. `non div.` (every player takes multiple stops). Avoid long unsplit triple/quadruple stops (`violin/cello` bow rolls chord bottom-to-top).

## 4. Practical Wind, Brass & Percussion Idioms (Berlioz/Strauss, Widor, Prout & Absil)

| Family | Instrument | Practical Sounding Range (Absil / Classics) | Golden Sweet Spot & Critical Pitfall Guard |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Winds** | **Piccolo** | `D5 – C8` | Shines above `C6`; weak & breathy `D5–G5`; piercing shriek above `G7` |
| | **Flute / Alto Flute** | `B3 – C7` (`G3–G6` alto) | Warm velvety `C4–G4` (easily buried!), agile & brilliant `G4–G6` |
| | **Oboe / English Horn** | `Bb3 – G6` (`E3–C6` EH) | Plaintive vocal `F4–C6`; **low `Bb3–D4` coarse/honking—never write `pp` here!** |
| | **Clarinet (Bb/A/Bass)** | `D3 – Bb6` (`Bb1–G5` Bass Cl) | **4 distinct registers**: Chalumeau (`D3–F4` dark liquid), Throat (`F#4–Bb4` pale/weak), Clarion (`B4–C6` luminous vocal), Altissimo (`C#6+` shrill) |
| | **Bassoon / Contrabassoon** | `Bb1 – Eb5` (`Bb0–Bb3` Cbn) | Rich, dry comedic/expressive tenor `G3–D4`; strong bass anchor only at `p–mf` (`ff` overpowered by trombones/tuba) |
| **Brass** | **French Horn (in F)** | `B1 – F5` | **Master blender** uniting woods + strings; warm heroic `F3–C5`; stopped (`+` / *bouché*) metallic bite; mark 1 degree louder than trumpets/bones in tutti (Rimsky) |
| | **Trumpet (Bb)** | `E3 – C6` (pro up to `D6/F6`) | Incisive hero `G4–A5`; avoid sluggish low `E3–G3`; mutes (Straight/Cup/Harmon) transform volume & brightness drastically |
| | **Tenor & Bass Trombone** | `E2 – Bb4` / `Bb0 – F4` | Purest homogeneous brass harmony (`G2–G4`); glissando works ONLY across 1st–7th slide harmonics |
| | **Bass Tuba** | `D1 – F4` | Firm velvety foundational bass (`F1–C3`); never muddy fast runs in `p` sub-octave |
| **Percussion** | **Timpani** | `D2 – A3` (classic set 4–5 drums) | Needs retuning pauses; roll with soft mallets for crescendo tension (`p` -> `ff`) without cluttering harmony |

- **Symphonic String Headcount Ratios (Piston & Absil)**: Classic full `16 VnI / 14 VnII / 12 Va / 10 Vc / 8 Cb` vs. Chamber/Studio `10–8–6–6–4` -> scales both dynamic ceiling and legato blend.
