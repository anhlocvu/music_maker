# Perceptual Timbre Grouping (ACTOR / McAdams), Belkin & Modern Studio Scoring

## 1. Taxonomy of Orchestral Grouping Effects (TOGE — McAdams, Goodchild & Soden)

Orchestration works by exploiting how human auditory scene analysis groups acoustic energy across three temporal hierarchies:

### Hierarchy 1: Concurrent Grouping (*Vertical Event Formation*)
Determines whether simultaneous instruments fuse into a single timbre (**Blend**) or stay perceptually separate (**Non-Blend**):
- **Blend Acoustic Drivers**:
  1. **Onset & Offset Synchrony** (`<= 30–50 ms` arrival synchrony locks partial tracks together into one perceptual event).
  2. **Harmonicity** (harmonic frequency ratios fuse cleanly; inharmonic bells/gong resist fusion).
  3. **Parallel Pitch & Dynamic Envelopes** (matched vibrato rate/phase and crescendo contours enhance blend; independent vibratos break fusion!).
  4. **Spectral Envelope Overlap & Centroid Match** (similar formant regions e.g. French Horn + Bassoon fuse tightly; Oboe + Flute stays brighter on top).
- **Two Distinct Blend Subtypes (Sandell / ACTOR)**:
  1. **Timbral Augmentation**: One dominant carrier instrument is reinforced/colored by a subservient partner (e.g., Cellos carrying melody embellished softly `pp` by Bass Clarinet or Horn).
  2. **Timbral Emergence**: Two or more balanced voices fuse equally to synthesize an *entirely new hybrid timbre* unrecognizable as either source alone (e.g., Flute + Clarinet unison, or Horn + Cello + Viola octave-unison hybrid!).
- **Non-Blend (`Timbral Heterogeneity`)**: Intentional mismatch of onset (`staccato pizz` vs `legato woodwind`), register, vibrato, or spectral brightness so voices remain audibly distinct (**Attack–Sustain** clarity).

### Hierarchy 2: Sequential Grouping (*Horizontal Streams & Stratification*)
- **Stream Integration vs. Segregation**: Repeating compatible timbres/registers every step binds into a continuous horizontal melody (*Klangfarbenmelodie* works when spectral steps stay close!); sudden octave leaps or brass-to-woodwind jumps split into segregated streams.
- **Orchestral Stratification (Foreground / Middleground / Background)**: Separate strata by coordinating timbre + articulation *within* each stratum while maximizing timbral + dynamic + rhythmic contrast *across* strata.

### Hierarchy 3: Segmental Grouping (*Boundary Chunking & Form*)
- **Timbral Shift**: Re-scoring a repeated phrase slightly to refresh color while preserving motif continuity.
- **Timbral Echo**: Repeating a phrase or fragment quieter/farther (`Trumpet -> Flute`, `Oboe -> muted Trumpet` / offstage effect) to create spatial depth (**Rimsky-Korsakov "affinity"**).
- **Antiphonal Contrast**: Call-and-response dialogue paired with harmonic polarity (`Tonic <-> Dominant`) across opposing instrument groups (e.g., Winds vs. Strings).
- **Timbral Juxtaposition**: Rapid back-and-forth alternation between contrasting equal-weight blocs (e.g., Sibelius low-brass vs. treble-strings tension surge).
- **Sectional Boundaries**: Coordinating massed orchestration shifts, register expansion, and dynamic arrivals to articulate large-scale form transitions (Emily Dolan's orchestral curves).

## 2. Alan Belkin's "Composing with Timbres" & Registral Principles

1. **Plane-of-Tone Principle**: Elements placed in separate pitch registers with a wide empty gap are NOT heard on the same structural plane—use wide gaps purposefully for playful/ethereal space (`Type II` solo + high chimes), and fill the **middle octave (`C3–C5`)** continuously when grandeur and warmth are required.
2. **Registral Saturation**: Fullness demands balanced partial density across low-mid harmonics; never judge simulation loudness blindly from raw samples—audit balance horizontally against room decay (`c80_db`) and articulation weight.

## 3. Modern Film, Pops & Studio Orchestration Practice (Tim Davies *deBreved*)

Bridging live studio/hybrid scoring and MIDI/SoundFont realizations cleanly:

- **Studio Brass & Wind Order vs. Concert Hall**:
  - In **session / film scores**, write **Horns in strict pitch order (`1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6` top-down)** rather than classical interlocking (`1, 3, 2, 4`)—simplifies fast sight-reading and stand edits; keep Concert Hall scores interlocked unless specified.
  - Condense double or triple woodwinds onto balanced staves (`2` per family max unless hyper-complex); group **Trumpet 1 alone** on staff 1 (carries solos/high rests cleanly) and Trumpets 2–3 on staff 2; Trombones 1–2 on staff 1, Bass Trombone on staff 2.
  - **Horn Voicing Rule**: If scoring 4 horns on a 3-note chord, **double the top voice**—adds warmth and fullness without thickening low mud; keep horns out of the low register (`< C3`) when clarity is needed.
- **Key Signatures & Concert Scores**:
  - Modern pure film cues typically omit key signatures (explicit accidentals reduce sight-reading errors) in **concert pitch** (`Piccolo, Contrabassoon, Glockenspiel, Xylophone, Celesta, Guitar, Double Bass` retain written octave transpositions).
  - Conversely, **Song / Pops / Jazz-Hybrid charts** ALWAYS use **key signatures** and **Guide Staves** showing rhythm section, synth, bass, and vocal melody cues so orchestral entries lock dynamically to the band.
- **Strategic Silence & Pops Survival Rules**:
  - **Do NOT double the band low-end by default**: Leave Bassoons and low strings tacet in heavy pop/hip-hop/rock verses where Electric/Synth Bass + Kick already command `35–140 Hz`. Save Bassoon/Cello-Bass octave doubling for naked acoustic breaks or huge *tutti* peaks.
  - **Mute Overlapping Samples**: Never stack orchestral brass/strings on top of pre-existing synth-orchestral pads in a backing track; mute the synth/guide orchestra layer first.
  - **Reversible Form Additions**: When orchestrating existing songs, keep Verse 1–2 spacious (even resting the full orchestra on Verse 1!) and scale Chorus 1 -> Chorus 2 -> Chorus 3 (`orchestrate top-down, then thin Chorus 1`). Use dedicated **Intros** or **Interludes** to spotlight orchestra cleanly without disrupting vocal timing.
  - **Woodwind Motion**: Replace static whole-note wind pads with **arpeggiator waves** or **dovetailed staggered overlaps** (rotating instruments breathe effortlessly without audible seams!).
