# Approach Tones, Enclosures, Melodic Devices & Error Recovery

Turn static arpeggios into fluent melodic lines by wrapping chord tones with approach tones, directional pivots, quartal intervals, and authentic ornaments.

## 1. Approach-Tone Taxonomy (Bob Keller & Simon Purcell)

Never restrict a solo purely to vertical chord tones (`1, 3, 5, 7`) or long scale climbs. Target chord/color tones on goal beats and lead into them via structured approaches:

| Technique | Pattern | Target Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Single Chromatic Below** | `Target - 1 semitone -> Target` | Strongest forward pull into `1, 3, 5, 7` or `9` |
| **Single Diatonic Above** | `Scale tone above -> Target` | Smooth downward cantabile resolution |
| **Chromatic Multiple Approach (2–3 notes)** | Chromatic walk up (`e.g. ♯2 -> ♮3 -> 4 -> ♯4 -> 5`) or down (`♭7 -> 6 -> ♭6 -> 5`) landing on beat 1/3 target | Generates signature bebop drive across barlines |
| **Scale Sequential Fragments** | Short 3–5 note scale cells aligned so chord tones fall on-beat and tensions fall off-beat (`appoggiaturas`) | Smooth forward momentum without aimless wandering |

## 2. Enclosure Patterns (Simon Purcell & Bob Keller)

An **enclosure** frames a target chord tone from **both sides** (above and below) before landing squarely on the target pitch:

1. **Diatonic-Above -> Chromatic-Below -> Target** (e.g., into `E` on `Cmaj7`: `F -> D♯ -> E`)
2. **Chromatic-Above -> Chromatic-Below -> Target** (e.g., into `G` on `Cmaj7`: `A♭ -> F♯ -> G`)
3. **Below -> Above -> Target** (reverse approach for rhythmic surprise, e.g., `F♯ -> A -> G`)
4. **Extended 3- or 4-Note Enclosures**: Double step above -> half-step below -> target (`G -> F -> D♯ -> E`), often phrased across an upbeat into beat 1 or beat 3.

### Four-Step Enclosure Practice/Generation Pipeline
1. Lock the chord-tone skeleton (`3` and `7` per bar).
2. Add single below/above approach tones preceding downbeat targets.
3. Upgrade selected barline targets into 2- to 4-note enclosures.
4. Vary metric placement so targets also land on **and-of-2**, **beat 3**, or **and-of-4** anticipations.

## 3. Directional Craft, Quartal Melodies & Ornaments (Bob Keller & Mark Watkins)

Prevent scalar predictability by injecting deliberate contour shifts:
- **Direction Changes & Skip / Zig-Zag**: Alternate ascending leaps (`3rd`, `4th`, `5th`, `6th`) with contrary stepwise resolution (`leap up -> step down`, or `step up -> leap down`) so every phrase bends organically.
- **Quartal Melodies (`4ths`)**: Stack consecutive perfect or augmented 4ths (`e.g. D -> G -> C -> F`) for open, modern modal color over `m7`, `7sus4`, or `maj7♯11` chords.
- **Turns (Charlie Parker Rhythmic Alignment)**: Ornament sustained target notes with a rapid upper-neighbor turn executed as a **16th-note triplet + 8th note** locked to the underlying swing triplet subdivision (Watkins Ch. 3).
- **Repetition, Sequencing & Quoting**:
  - Repeat an exact rhythmic/melodic hook over shifting harmony (or transpose a sequence across consecutive bricks).
  - Insert brief, tasteful melodic **quotes** from standard repertoire as structural signposts.
- **Duration Variation**: Break uniform 8th-note chains using quarters, dotted quarters, half notes, triplets, and sharp short stops (`The Bop`).
- **Off-Beat Phrase Starts**: Begin phrases on upbeats (`and-of-1`, `and-of-4`) rather than mechanically striking Beat 1.

## 4. Avoid-Note Rules & Live Error-Recovery Failsafe (Bob Keller)

### Avoid-Note Constraint
- Over Major (`maj7`, `6`), the **natural 4th** forms a dissonant minor 9th against the major 3rd; never sustain it as a target tone—restrict it to a rapid passing tone (`<=` 8th note) resolving downward to the 3rd or upward through `♯4` to the 5th.

### Mandatory Error-Recovery Rule (Improvisational Failsafe)
> **Rule**: *Never sustain a wrong or clashing non-chord tone.*
- If a solo line lands unexpectedly on a dissonant off-key pitch:
  1. Keep its duration short (treat it retroactively as a passing/appoggiatura accent).
  2. **Immediately resolve by half-step or whole-step** into an adjacent safe chord tone (`1, 3, 5, 7`) or color tone (`6, 9, maj7`), effectively converting the "mistake" into a deliberate **chromatic approach tone**.
  3. Optionally repeat that exact "chromatic inflection -> resolution" motif one beat later to convince the listener it was an intentional compositional device!
