# Melodic Cells, Permutations, Sequences & Bebop Lines

Construct dense, idiomatic lines quickly by permuting compact 4-note cells, linking `ii-V` pairs, applying Bebop scales with upbeat passing alignment, and deploying controlled side-stepping.

## 1. 4-Note Cell Vocabulary (Mark Watkins, Ch. 12)

Instead of improvising raw scalar steps, build phrases from crisp **4-note pitch cells** (analogous to intervallic building blocks):

### Cell Types
1. **`1-3-5` & Octave Triad Cells**: Built strictly from root, 3rd, 5th, and octave pitches in 4-note rhythmic windows.
2. **`1-5` 4-Note Cells**: Root-to-fifth diatonic patterns framing the bottom half of the chord-scale (`1, 2, 3, 4, 5` selections in 4-note cells).
3. **`7th` Cells**: Inject active `6th` and `7th` degrees for linear propulsion across barlines:
   - **`♭7` resolution law**: `♭7` pulls **downward** (to the `3rd` of the next chord in root-by-5th cycles).
   - **`maj7` resolution law**: `maj7` pulls **upward** (toward `root` or ascending continuation).
4. **Upper-Half `1-5 as 5-9` Cells**: Transpose a `1-5` cell shape to start on the **5th degree** of the target chord so it outlines `5 - 6 - 7 - 9` (the upper structure!).
   - *Key versatility shortcut*: **Minor `1-5` cells** fit seamlessly over **both `m7` chords** (from root/5th) **and `dom7` chords** (outlining `5-6-♭7-9` or altered upper colors depending on placement).

### Four Systematic Cell Permutations
Any 4-note cell yields four distinct melodic profiles without changing its underlying chord-scale identity:
- **Prime (`P`)**: Original pitch-contour ordering
- **Inversion (`I`)**: Intervals inverted (ascending leaps become descending steps of equal interval size, etc.)
- **Retrograde (`R`)**: Reversed time ordering (`note 4 -> 3 -> 2 -> 1`)
- **Retrograde-Inversion (`RI`)**: Combined reversal and contour inversion.

## 2. Chaining Cells: Circular Patterns, Octave Displacement & `ii-V` Pairing

- **Circular Patterns (Tonal / Diatonic Permutations)**: Sequence 4-note patterns across four directional contour classes within the current diatonic mode:
  - **`up / up`** (step/arpeggio up, then next cell up)
  - **`up / down`** (ascend then descend)
  - **`down / down`** (continuous descending cascade)
  - **`down / up`** (descend then rebound upward)
- **Chaining Across Barlines & Octave Displacement**: Connect consecutive cells smoothly via `7 -> 3` or `5 -> 9` common/step transitions; break single-octave predictability by displacing a cell's tail or next cell by **`±12` semitones** (`octave displacement`).
- **`ii - V` Cell Pairing Strategy**: Start the **`ii7` cell on its 3rd degree** (`♭3` of `ii`), propelling the contour naturally through its upper extensions straight into the guide tone/altered color of `V7`.
- **Canonical Bebop 4-Note Dominant Cells** (resolving cleanly over `V7 -> I`):
  - **`3 - ♭9 - ♭7 - maj7 -> 5th of I`**
  - **`3 - ♭9 - maj7 - ♭7 -> 3rd of I`**
  *(Note how both embed the iconic `3 -> ♭9` diminished-outline leap followed by an enclosure-like pull onto `5th` or `3rd` of tonic `I`.)*

## 3. Side-Stepping / Side-Slipping ("Playing Outside")

Create sophisticated harmonic tension over static vamps or modal pedals without losing coherence:
- **Tonal vs. Real Transposition**:
  - *Tonal transposition* shifts a cell within the parent scale (changes exact intervals to fit the key).
  - **Real transposition** preserves the **exact semitone interval structure** while shifting pitch center up or down by **a half-step (`±1 st`)** or along a root sequence (e.g., cycling up in minor 3rds or whole steps).
- **Execution Protocol**: Establish the "inside" cell clearly for 1–2 beats -> **side-slip by `+1` or `-1` semitone** using real transposition -> resolve crisply **back inside** on a strong beat chord tone (`1, 3, 5, 7`) at the barline or phrase end.

## 4. Bebop Scales & Downbeat-Chord-Tone Alignment (Aebersold / Red Book & David Baker)

Standard 7-note scales misalign chord tones onto off-beats during steady 8th-note runs. **Bebop scales** insert **1 strategic chromatic passing tone** (making an 8-note scale) so chord tones (`1, 3, 5, 7/6`) always remain anchored on downbeats:

| Bebop Scale | Added Chromatic Passing Tone | Primary Target Harmony |
|---|---|---|
| **Bebop Major** | `♯5` / `♭6` (between `5` and `6`) | `maj7`, `maj6` (`I`, `IV`) |
| **Bebop Dominant** | `nat7` / `maj7` (between `♭7` and `1`) | `dom7`, `ii-V7` (`V7`) |
| **Bebop Minor** | `nat3` (between `♭3` and `4`) | `m7` (`ii`, `i`, modal Dorian) |
| **Bebop Half-Diminished** | `nat5` (between `♭5` and `♭6`) | `m7♭5` (`iiø7`) |

### The Mandatory Upbeat Alignment Rule
- When launching continuous descending or ascending 8th-note lines from any primary chord tone (**`1`, `3`, `5`, or `7`**) on a strong downbeat, **the added chromatic passing tone MUST fall on the upbeat** (`&`).
- Conversely, if starting a Bebop scale on a non-chord extension (`2, 4, 6`), insert an extra half-step passing inflection or triplet turn so target chord tones re-align onto downbeats (`1` and `3`).
- Combine with David Baker's Bebop hallmarks (from *Red Book*): double-time feel bursts, chromatic enclosures on `3` and `5`, and asymmetrical phrase lengths crossing 4-bar boundaries.
