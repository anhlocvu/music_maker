## 1. Jazz & Contemporary Voicing / Voice-Leading Rules

### Acoustic Spacing, Doubling & Omission (OMTv2 *Jazz Voicings*)
- **Harmonic-Series Spacing**: Place **wide intervals in lower registers** (octave, 5th) and **closer intervals in upper registers** to avoid low-end mud.
- **Keep Extensions High**: Voice **9ths, 11ths, and 13ths above the 7th** (a 13th voiced below the 7th sounds like a 6th, not a 13th).
- **Doubling**: If doubling a pitch, double the **bass note** or the **chord root**.
- **Omissions**:
  - **Omit the 5th** freely—partial 3 merely reinforces the root and adds little color.
  - **Omit the root** only when a bass instrument supplies it (otherwise keep root in left hand). Hardly ever omit the **3rd** or **7th**.
- **"Lazy" Voice Leading**: Keep **common tones** stationary and move remaining upper voices by **step (1–2 semitones)** whenever possible, reserving leaps for same-chord motion or deliberate contrast.

### The `3–7` and `9–13` Upper-Voice Paradigms
Assuming bass plays the root:
1. **Two Upper Voices (`3–7` Paradigm)**:
   - Place the **3rd and 7th** above the bass (omitting the 5th).
   - **Root motion by 5th (`ii–V–I`)**: One voice alternates `3rd -> 7th -> 3rd` while the other alternates `7th -> 3rd -> 7th` (moving smoothly by half/whole step; intervals remain 4ths/5ths).
   - **Root motion by 2nd (`V–IV`)**: Both voices move in parallel (`3rd -> 3rd`, `7th -> 7th`).
2. **Three Upper Voices (`9–13` Extension Line)**:
   - Keep `3–7` in the lower two upper voices and add a **top voice alternating `9th -> 13th -> 9th`** over 5th-related roots (starting from diatonic key tones, then altering to `b9/#9/b13` where surrounding chords call for tension).

## 2. Adding Embellishing Chords & Adjusting Harmonic Rhythm

When adding passing/embellishing chords to a progression, **subdivide the duration** of the prolonged or preceding chord (harmonic rhythm adjustment) based on melodic space:
1. **Applied `V7` Insertion**: Split a 1-bar chord (`Bb` for 4 beats before `C7`) into `Bb (2 beats) – G7 (2 beats) -> C7`.
2. **Applied `ii7` Insertion**: Prefix any dominant chord with its `mi7` or `ø7` a 5th above (`C7` split into `Gm7 – C7`).
3. **Common-Tone Diminished Seventh (`CT°7`)**:
   - **Construction**: Keep the **root of the embellished chord as a common tone** and move all other voices by half/whole step to form a `°7` chord containing that root.
   - **Placement**: Either insert mid-chord (`Bb – Bb_CT°7 – Bb`) OR **displace beat 1** by jumping straight into `CT°7` on beat 1 (`C_CT°7 -> C7`). `CT°7` prolongs harmony via neighbor motion without changing Roman numeral function.

## 3. Jazz Substitutions & `ii–V` Space

- **Major vs. Minor `ii–V–I`**: Major uses `ii7 – V7 – Imaj7` (`mi7–7–ma7`); Minor uses `iiø7 – V7 – i7` (`ø7–7–mi7`, keeping `V` major/dominant). Variants substitute `I6` (`Misty`) or `V7+` / `V7b5` (`Prelude to a Kiss`, `A Night in Tunisia`).
- **Applied Chords in Turnarounds**: In fifth-wise progressions (`I – vi – ii – V`), convert minor chords into `V7` on the same root (`I – VI7 – II7 – V7`) or prefix `ii` with `ii/ii–V/ii`.
- **Mode Mixture via `le` (`b^6`)**: In major keys, swap `ii7 -> iiø7` and `V7 -> V7b9` to inject `le` (`b^6`) without altering harmonic function (e.g., *All of You*, *Satin Doll*).
- **Tritone Substitution (`subV7`)**: Replace a **resolving** `V7` (diatonic or applied) with the `dom7` a tritone away, sharing the identical tritone (`3rd & 7th` swapped). **Best used when resolving down by minor 2nd** (`Db7 -> Cmaj7`)—avoid substituting non-resolving dominants blindly. In turnarounds, tritone-sub every other `V7` for a chromatic descending bass (`Cmaj7 – Eb7 – Dm7 – Db7 – Cmaj7`).
- **McClimon `ii–V` Space Transformations**: Move smoothly between key centers by converting `Imaj7 -> ii7` (lowering the 7th then shifting root/quality, e.g., whole-step descending `ii–V–I` chains like *Afternoon in Paris* `Cmaj7 -> Cm7–F7 -> Bbmaj7 -> Bbm7–Eb7 -> Abmaj7`).

## 4. Tonicization, Closely Related Modulation & Modal Mixture

### Tonicization (`V(7)/x` & `vii°(7)/x`)
- Any major or minor triad (`I, ii, iii, IV, V, vi` in major; `i, III, iv, v/V, VI, VII` in minor) can be tonicized; **never** tonicize diminished/augmented triads (`vii°`, `ii°`).
- **Think as Altered Diatonic Chords**: `ii` with `fa -> fi` (`^4 -> #^4`) becomes `II# = V/V`; `I` with added `te` (`b^7`) becomes `V7/IV`; `I` with raised `#^1` and `b^7` becomes `vii°7/ii`.
- **Resolving into 7th Chords**: When `V7/x` resolves to another 7th chord (`D7 -> G7 -> C`), its secondary leading tone can slip **down by semitone** into the 7th (`F# -> F♮ -> E`).
- **Deceptive Highlight**: `V -> vii°7/vi -> vi` intensifies deceptive motion.

### Extended Tonicization & Closely Related Modulation
- **1 Home + 5 Closely Related Keys (1-Accidental Grid, 6 keys total)**: Home key + 1-sharp key + 1-flat key, plus their 3 relative minor/major keys (corresponding to the 6 consonant triads `I, ii, iii, IV, V, vi` in major; `i, III, iv, v, VI, VII` in minor). Most common targets: `I -> V` or `I -> vi` in major; `i -> III` or `i -> v` in minor.
- **Direct (Phrase) Modulation**: Enter the new key abruptly at a phrase boundary (e.g., Verse -> Chorus or final-chorus lift).
- **Pivot Chord Modulation**:
  - **Best Pivot**: A shared chord that is **Predominant in both keys** (`iv7` becomes `ii7`, or `ii` becomes `vi`/`iv` leading straight into `V`).
  - **2nd Best Pivot**: Tonic in old key becoming Predominant in new key (`I` becomes `IV`).
  - **Avoid**: Using `V -> I` as a pivot (contradictory stability).
  - **Firm Arrival**: Avoid cadencing too fast right after the pivot; use one deceptive evasion before the confirming PAC in the new key.

### Modal Mixture (Borrowing from Parallel Mode)
- Borrowing changes **chord quality, not harmonic function**.
- **Most Common in Major**:
  - Keeping original root (`le = b^6` only): `ii°6`, `iiø7`, `iv`.
  - Lowered roots (`me = b^3`, `te = b^7`, `le = b^6`): `bVI`, `bIII`, `bVII`, and minor `i` (`v` in major usually signals modulation rather than brief mixture).
- **Consistency Rule**: Borrow **full chords** from parallel minor rather than awkward partial mixtures (`mi` + `le` together), and once mixture enters a predominant span, sustain it until the harmony moves to the dominant category.
- **Distant Modulation via Mixture**: Use a mixture chord as a chromatic pivot (e.g., `iv` in `Bb` minor-mixture = `Ebmi` becomes `ii` in `Db` major).
- **Picardy Third**: End a minor-key phrase or piece with a major `I` triad.
