# Ted Greene Chord Substitution, Approach & Harmonic Improvement Toolkit

Distilled strictly from Ted Greene's pedagogical sheets (*Chord Substitution Parts 1–10*, 1973; *Harmonic Improvement Parts 1–5*, 1975–1976; *Common Harmonic Improvement Concepts*, 1979; *Chord Progressions – What to Expect in Popular Music*, 1974; *Harmonic Devices for Gospel Feels*, 1977; *Scale-Chord Relationships*, 1974; *Harmonic Tendencies* & *Summary of Modern Jazz Chord Movements*, 1977).

> **Ted Greene Golden Rule of Melody & Moderation**:
> *"The top note (melody note) in these chords plays a big part in your ears' acceptance or rejection of these sounds, so watch closely what is going on with the melody."* Never force `b5` or chromatic substitutions where they clash with the melody—music is about finding convincing sounds, not playing intellectual games.

---

## 1. Three Chord Families & Top-Note Compatibility

Any chord belongs to one of three parent families (**Major**, **Minor**, **Dominant 7th**) plus **Diminished 7th**. Sibling extensions within the same family can substitute for their parent chord **only when the melody note on top is compatible**:
- **Major Family** (`maj, 6, maj7, /9, 2, maj9, 6/9, maj13, 6/9#11, maj9#11, maj/#11, maj7+, maj9+`): Fits smoothly as a substitute for any major triad **not functioning as a dominant or dominant of a dominant (`V/V`, etc.)**—namely `I`, `IV` (in major keys), `bII`, `bIII`, `bVI`, `bVII` (rarer `bV`). On `II, V, VI` prefer `/9` or `2` (add9/sus2); `sus` fits all degrees; `6` or `6/9` rarely on other degrees. Be cautious with `#11` or `+5` unless the melody supports `#4` / `#5` (`maj7+, maj9+` can color `7+` sounds).
- **Minor Family (4 Subgroups)**:
  1. *Standard (`m, m7, m7/11, m9, m11, m/9`)*: Replaces any `m` or `m7`.
  2. *Half-Diminished / Augmented Minor (`m7b5, m7b5/11, m7+`)*: `m7b5` types replace `m7` and are most common on `ii` of minor (`vii` of major); identical in notes to `m6` a minor 3rd higher (`F#m7b5 = Am6`). Try `m7+` for `7#9+`.
  3. *Minor 6th (`m6, m6/9, m6/7, m6/9/7, m6/11, m6/9#11`)*: Replaces `i`, `iv`, `bvi` (temporary `i` tonicizations), or `ii` (`m6, m6/11`; rare on `biii, vi, bvii`); can replace a `dom7` a **1/2 step lower** for altered color (`Am6` for `G#7`).
  4. *Line-Cliché (`m -> m(maj7) -> m7 -> m6`)*: Use for moving inner lines or when `maj7` sits in the melody.
- **Dominant 7th Family (5 Subgroups)**:
  1. *Natural/Pretty (`7, 7/6, 9, 13`)*: Gentle/warm or bluesy; verify top note!
  2. *Suspended (`7sus, 7/6sus, 11, 13sus`)*: Virtually identical to companion `m7` a 5th higher (`G13sus <-> Dm9`); works almost everywhere Group 1 works.
  3. *Whole-Tone & Melodic-Minor 5th-Up (`+, 7+, 7b5, 9+` and `9b5, #11 [9#11], 13#11, 7b5`)*: Whole-tone replaces `V7` or `V/V`; Melodic-Minor 5th-up (`9b5/#11`) fits `I` (endings), `bII` (sub for `V7`), `II` (for `II7` in major keys), occasionally `bIII` (in major keys for `VI7`), `IV` (for `IV7`), `#IV/bV` (for `I7`), `bVI` (for `II7`/`bVI7`), `bVII` (for `II7`/`bVII7`), and rarely `V` (for `V7`).
  4. *Altered Resolving (`7b9, 7#9, 7b9+, 7b9b5, 7#9+, 7#9b5, 13b9, 13#9, 13b9b5`)*: Replaces resolving `V7`.
  5. *Minor-V / `11b9` (`11b9, 11b9+, 7/11, 7/6/11`)*: Almost exclusively on `V` for `V7` (rarely `I` or `II`).

## 2. The 9 Harmonic Improvement Concepts

### Concept 1 — Chord Enrichment, Siblings & Abbreviation
Expand plain triads (`C -> Cmaj9`) or reduce cluttered 7ths to clean triads when needed (`C#7 -> C#` or `G#m7b5 -> G#°`).

### Concept 2 — 1/2-Step Approaches & Polar Moving-Line Chords
- **From Above** (most common): Precede any chord with a similar chord a half-step above (`Db9` or `Dbmaj7` or `C#m7/11 -> Cm7`).
- **From Below**: Precede from a half-step below (`Bm9` or `B7/6` or `Bmaj7 -> Cm7`).
- **Polar 1/2-Step Moving-Line Chords**: Bass approaches the target from a **1/2 step below**, while upper voices approach from a **1/2 step above** (contrary motion). Melodic leaps in the soprano often pair well here. Apply with moderation.

### Concept 3 — 5th Approaches ("Back-Cycling") & Sandwich Inserts
Precede any major, minor, or dominant chord with a chord whose root is a **5th above**:
- Preceding `Cmaj9`: try `G11`, `G7+`, `Gm9`, or `Gmaj7`.
- Preceding `C9`: try `Gm9`, `Gm7b5`, `G7b9+`. Preceding `Cm9`: try `Gm7`, `Gm6`, `G7+`.
- **Sandwich / Alternating Inserts**: Split 1–2 bars of a static dominant (`A7 | A7 | D7`) by inserting its companion `m7` a 5th higher (`A7 Em7 | A7 Em7 | D7 Am7`).

### Concept 4 — `b5` Substitution ("Cross-Cycle" / Tritone Sub)
Replace a `dom7` chord with another `dom7` a **tritone (`b5`) away** because they share the same **3rd and `b7th` essence** (`D7` has `F#, C`; `Ab7` has `C, Gb`).
- **Best Chord Choices**: Major-scale extended dominants (`7/6, 9, 13`) and overtone dominants (`9b5, #11, 13#11`) work best as `b5` substitutes, sounding like an altered dominant on the original root.
- **Extended `b5` Chains (`III7 – VI7 – II7 – V7` Variants)**:
  - `bVII7 – bIII7 – bVI7 – bII7` (all `b5` subs; note the uplifting effect of `bIII7` for `VI7`)
  - `bVII7 – VI7 – bVI7 – V7` (chromatic descending bass)
  - `III7 – bIII7 – II7 – bII7` (alternating diatonic & `b5` subs)
  - `III7 – bIII7 – bVImaj7 – ii7 – V7`
  - Add `ii7` before each dominant: `viim7–III7 – bvii7–bIII7 – vi7–II7 – bvi7–bII7`, or `iv7–bVII7 – iii7–VI7 – biii7–bVI7 – ii7–V7`.
- **11 Essential Cycle Patterns (Major Key Reference)**:
  1. `Imaj7 – IVmaj7 – viiø7 – III7 – vi7 – II7 – ii7 – V7`
  2. `I7 – IV7 – III7 – VI7 – II7 – bVI7 – ii7 – V7`
  3. `Imaj7 – iv7 – bVII7 – bIIImaj7 – bVImaj7 – bIImaj7 – ii7 – V7`
  4. `Imaj7 – (iv7) – bvii7 – bIII7 – bVImaj7 – bIImaj7 – ii7 – V7`
  5. `I7 – IV7 – bVII7 – bIII7 – bVI7 – bIImaj7 – ii7 – V7`
  6. `Imaj7 – IVmaj7 – viiø7 – III7 – vi7 – II7 – v7 – I7 (-> IV)`
  7. `Imaj7 – iv7 – iii7 – VI7 – biii7 – bVI7 – ii7 – V7`
  8. `#ivø7 – VII7 – iiiø7 – VI7 – iiø7 – V7 – I`
  9. `Imaj7 – I7 – IVmaj7 – iv7 – iii7 – VI7 – ii7 – V7`
  10. `Imaj7 – IVmaj7 – #ivø7 – VII7 – iii7 – VI7 – ii7 – V7`
  11. `Imaj7 – #ivø7 (or IV7) – VII7 – iii7 – VI7 – ii7 – V7 – bIImaj7`

### Concept 5 — 3rd Substitution (Common-Tone Thirds)
Replace, precede, or follow chords whose roots are a **3rd apart**:
- `Major` ↔ `m7` a m3 lower (`vi`) or M3 higher (`iii`): `Cmaj7` ↔ `Am7` or `Em7`.
- `m7` ↔ `Major` a M3 lower or m3 higher: `Am7` ↔ `F/9` or `Cmaj9`; `Em7` ↔ `C` or `Gmaj7`.
- `m7b5` ↔ `dom7` a M3 lower (`Bm7b5` ↔ `G13`), or `minor` a m3 higher (`Bm7b5` ↔ `Dm7` / `Dm9–G9`).
- *Rare `dom7` -> `m7b5` (M3 higher)*: `Dm7 – G7 – Cmaj7` → `IV7 – viiø7 – iii7 – vi7 – ii7 – V7 – I7`.
- *Other pairs*: `ii7` ↔ `IVmaj7`, `V7` ↔ `viiø7`, `ii(7)` ↔ `viiø7`, `v(7)` ↔ `iiiø7`, `iv(7)` ↔ `iiø7`, `#ivø7` ↔ `vi / II7`, `bVI` ↔ `iv`.

### Concept 6 — Companion Substitution (`m7` ↔ `dom7` / `dom7sus` a 4th Higher)
- `Am7` and `D9` / `D13sus` share nearly all upper tones: replace `D9` (2 bars) with `D7 – Am7 – D9 – Am9 – D7`, or replace `Am7` with `D13sus`.
- `m6` (`Am6`) can be replaced with `dom7` (`9, 13, 9b5, #11, 13#11`) a **4th higher** (`D13`).

### Concept 7 — Quality / Family Change
Swap chord quality on the same root (`Am9` ↔ `A9`, `A6` -> `A13`, `Am/9` -> `Amaj9`). Because `3` vs. `b3` dramatically alters character, test against the melody carefully.

### Concept 8 — Scalewise & Parallel Embellishment
- **Diatonic Scalewise**: Precede a target with stepwise diatonic chords (`Am7 – D7` -> `Cmaj7 – Bm7 – Am7 – D7`; `G – C` -> `Gmaj7 – Am7 – Bm7 – Cmaj7`), optionally delaying the target chord or using **contrary motion** against the melody.
- **Chromatic & Parallel**: `C#m7 – Cm7 – Bm7 – E7 – Amaj7`, or parallel `dom7/6` descents (`B7 – Bb7/6 – A7 – Ab7/6 – G7 – Gb7/6 – F7 – E7/6 – Amaj7`).
- **Scale-to-Cycle Conversion**:
  - Descending scale (`Dmaj7 – C#m7 – Bm7 – Amaj7`) → Cycle (`Dmaj7 – G#ø7 – C#m7 – F#m7 – Bm7 – E7 – Amaj7`).
  - Ascending scale (`Bm7 – C#m7 – Dmaj7`) → Insert chords a 4th higher (`Bm7 – E7 – C#m7 – F#(m)7 – Dmaj7` [original sheet prints `D#∆7`]).
- **Combined Modern-Jazz Turnaround Examples** (combining `5th`, `b5`, and `1/2`-step approaches into `C`): `A7 – Dm7 – G7 – C`, `Am7 – Dm7 – G7 – C`, `Em7 – A7 – Dm7 – G7 – C`, `Ebm7 – Ab7 – Dm7 – G7 – C`, `Am7 – D7 – Dm7 – G7 – C`.

### Concept 9 — Diminished 7th Elision & Bass-Up Progressions
- **Same-Root `°7` Embellishment**: `A°7 -> A` (4-beat chord split `2+2`).
- **`°7` as Rootless `7b9` & Elision**: `A°7 = B7b9 = D7b9 = F7b9 = G#7b9`. In `B7 – E7 – A`, omitting `E7` yields `B7b9 -> A` (**elision**), accounting for direct `II7 -> I` and `bVI7 -> I` (`F9 -> A`) in popular music.
- **Building from the Bass Up**: Fix a melodic ascending bass line (e.g., `A – B – C – C#` or `B – C# – D – D#`) and fit any key-related chord above each bass note (`A – Bm7 – A°7 – A/C#`, `A – E7/B – A°7 – A/C#`, or `B7 – C#m7 – B°7 – B/D#`).

## 3. Popular Music Expectations & Gospel Harmonic Devices

### Popular Music Core Patterns (Ted Greene 1974)
- **Blues-ing `I` and `IV`**: Replace `I` or `IV` triads with `dom7` extensions (`I7`, `IV7`, `IV9b5`, `IV13#11`).
- **Secondary Sub-Dominants (`iiø7` before `V/minor`)**: In major, use `Em7b5 – A7 – Dm` (not only `Em7`), `F#m7b5 – B7 – Em`, `Bm7b5 – E7 – Am`.
- **Parallel Minor Borrowing in Major**: `C – Fm – Ab – C` or `C – Gm7 – C7 – F – Fm7 – Bb7 – C`.
- **Minor Keys**: Draw freely across **5 minor collections** (Natural, Harmonic, Melodic, Dorian, Phrygian), tonicizing `bII, bIII, iv, v, V, bVI, bVII`, and borrowing major `I` (Picardy) at phrase beginnings/ends.

### Gospel Harmonic Devices (Ted Greene 1977)
- `bVI – bIII – bVI – IV – (V11) – (I)` and `bIII – bVI – IV – IV7 (or V11) – I`
- Open/closed triads over **Bass Pedal (`R` or `5th`)** OR **Soprano/Inner Pedal (`R` or `5th`)**
- Bluesy/romantic colors: `#ivø7`, `bVI7`, `IV7`, same-root `dim°7` embellishments (`C°7 -> C`, `G°7 -> G`)
- `I – iii7 – IV – V` alternating with `I – III7 – vi – II7`
- Lydian/Suspended Gospel moves: `I – bVII6/9#11`, `bVII13sus`, `I–V7` and `I–I11/13sus` vamps, and **parallel triads moving up/down in minor 2nds, minor 3rds, or major 3rds** (plus *Bass-Enhanced Triads*).
