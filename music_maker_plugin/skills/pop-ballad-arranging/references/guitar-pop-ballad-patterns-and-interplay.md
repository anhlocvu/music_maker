# Guitar Patterns, Voicings & Ensemble Interplay for Pop Ballads

Grounded strictly in:
- Frans Absil, *Arranging by Examples* (§2.3.1 Guitar, p. 14; §5.4 Percussive Voicing)
- Jerry Tolson, *It's All About the Rhythm (Section)* (Midwest Clinic, p. 5)
- Dick Dunscomb & Bill Boris, *Keys to a Successful School Rhythm Section* (Midwest Clinic, pp. 7–8)
- U.S. Navy *Musician Volume 2* (NAVEDTRA 15026C, Section 3 Ch. 3, p. 144)
- TaiShun Tse, *Pop Ballad* (HK EDB, p. 23)
- Dave Stewart, *Arranging For Strings Part 2* (*Sound On Sound*, July 2012 — guitar open-string pedal interaction)
- John Walden, *Processing Acoustic Guitars* (*Sound On Sound*, Oct 2006)
- Big George, *Making Arrangements Part 3* (Peter Gunn on dual guitarists)

---

## 1. Non-Collision & Role Separation

1. **Never Comp Simultaneously with Piano** (*Tolson p. 5; US Navy p. 144; Absil p. 14*):
   - Guitar and piano must balance their harmonic contributions; **if the piano is actively comping/arpeggiating, the guitar should drop out, hold sparse color voicings, or switch to an alternate single-line/melodic role** (*Tolson p. 5*).
2. **Two Guitarists Must Play Distinct Rhythmic Parts** (*Peter Gunn, Making Arrangements Pt. 3*):
   - Never let two rhythm guitars strum the identical pattern; assign contrasting textures (e.g., low acoustic arpeggio vs. high electric clean figure or sparse strum).
3. **Minimal Broken-Chord Color in Piano-Led Ballads** (*HK EDB p. 23*):
   - In piano-and-strings ballads (e.g., *K歌之王* Chorus 2), use guitar (and harp) minimally for **broken chords** that add delicate rhythmic detail without dominating the texture.

---

## 2. Voicing Economy, Fretboard Ergonomics & Key Choice

1. **Compact Upper-String Voicings (3–4 Notes)** (*Tolson p. 5; Boris/Dunscomb p. 7*):
   - Keep rhythm guitar voicings in the **mid-range** concentrated on the **top four strings** (`D–G–B–E`), using **3–4 notes** (Freddie Green style even uses **2–3 notes per chord**) for clarity instead of thick 6-string barré chords.
   - Respect physical finger stretches and barré shifts across rapid chord changes; when needed, simplify or substitute chord shapes that retain essential chordal functions (`3rd & 7th` or upper colors—e.g., `Em7` over `C` bass instead of a cramped full `Cmaj9`, *Absil p. 14*).
2. **Tuning, Sharp Keys & Open-String Jangle** (*Absil p. 14; Dave Stewart Pt. 2*):
   - Standard tuning (`E2–A2–D3–G3–B3–E4`) strongly favors **sharp keys (`G, D, A, E`)** where open strings provide natural sustain and power-chord resonance (*Absil p. 14*).
   - **Open-String Pedal Resonance** (*Dave Stewart Pt. 2*): Keeping open top strings (`B3` and `E4`) ringing across changing bass roots (e.g., `Aadd2 -> Cmaj7 -> Fmaj7#4 -> Cmaj7/E`) creates shimmering ballad sustain.

## 3. Rhythm Lock, Signal Processing & Dynamic Discipline

1. **Attack Alignment with Bass & Drums** (*Boris/Dunscomb p. 8; US Navy p. 144*):
   - Align the pick attack on rhythmic chords with the **bass player's attack**, keeping rhythms clean, stylistic, and never overplayed.
2. **Specify Instrument Type, Role & Signal Processing** (*Absil p. 14; US Navy p. 144*):
   - Explicitly distinguish **Acoustic** vs. **Electric** guitar and **Rhythm** (chords, riffs, licks) vs. **Solo** roles.
   - For electric guitar, specify tone & signal processing (*clean*, *slap delay*, *long reverb*, *heavily distorted*) and enforce **dynamic pullback** (`End of solo` / lower dynamic) when returning to accompaniment so an amplified electric guitar never buries acoustic instruments (*Absil p. 14*).
3. **Ensemble Hits / Tutti Behavior** (*Absil §5.4*):
   - On percussive ensemble hits and tutti accents, **stop continuous strumming** and lock strictly to the accented unison/ensemble rhythm.

## 4. Mix Space & Acoustic Clarity (John Walden, *SOS* Oct 2006)

- In a full band mix (drums, bass, piano, vocal), roll off acoustic guitar lows **below 100 Hz** to prevent low-end mud, sweep/cut **100–300 Hz** (`2–3 dB`) if boxy/boomy, trim **1–3 kHz** slightly to clear room for the lead vocal, and add modest presence around **5–7 kHz** (preferable to >10 kHz sizzle when cymbals are busy).
- Gentle compression (`2:1`, `10–20 ms` attack to preserve pick/strum transient) or subtle L/R stereo doubling (checked in mono) helps sit strums cleanly around a centered vocal.
