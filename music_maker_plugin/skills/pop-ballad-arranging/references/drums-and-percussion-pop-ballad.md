# Drums & Percussion Craft for Pop Ballads

Grounded strictly in:
- Rory Dow, *Programming Realistic Drum Parts* (*Sound On Sound*, Sep 2010)
- Sam Inglis, *20 Tips On Creating Realistic Sequenced Drum Parts* (*Sound On Sound*, Oct 1999)
- Steve Fidyk, *Compositional Jazz Band Drumming* (Midwest Clinic, 2006)

## 1. Ballad Kit Restraint, Form Awareness & Tuning

1. **Dynamic Control & "Playing the Form"** (*Tolson p. 4; Dunscomb/Joseph pp. 5–6; Snake Davis Pt. 4*):
   - The drummer is the primary controller of **dynamics, mood, and section transitions**.
   - **Verse Restraint**: Hold the full kit out of Intro/Verse 1 (or use minimal kick/rim-shot/cross-stick and low-pass filtered close mics with room mics muted early on, *Mix Rescue*; Snake Davis swapped snare for **rim-shot** in Intro/Verse 1 so the Chorus snare thunders in!).
   - **Section Shifts**: Change pattern texture cleanly at form boundaries (e.g., closed hi-hat in Verse → open/semi-open hi-hat or **ride cymbal / ride bell** in Chorus/Solo, *Dow*; *Pete Thomas Pt. 4*).
2. **Subdivision & Pitch Tuning** (*Dunscomb p. 2; Pete Thomas Pt. 4*):
   - Subdivide mentally across slow ballad beats to prevent rushing or dragging.
   - **Tune drums/toms to the song's key** and match snare selection to the key/tonality (*Pete Thomas Pt. 4*) so kit resonances support rather than fight the bass and piano.
3. **Auxiliary Percussion Discipline** (*Tolson p. 5; US Navy p. 145; Peter Gunn Pt. 3; Dow*):
   - Use shakers, tambourines, or handclaps sparingly ("like spice") in **short 1–2 measure repeating patterns** locked to bass and drums; layering subtle live/humanized shaker or tambourine adds immediate organic lift to a ballad chorus.

## 2. 4-Limb Ergonomics & Realistic Kit Physics (*Dow 2010; Inglis 1999*)

1. **4-Limb Polyphony Cap**: A real drummer has two hands and two feet—never program kick + snare + 2 toms + ride + crash simultaneously, and **always mute hi-hats/ride cymbals during two-handed tom fills** (*Dow*, *Inglis Tip 1*).
2. **Sticking Consistency & Instrument Realism**:
   - Do not mix mutually incompatible tools in the same groove (e.g., brushed snare with stick rimshots, *Inglis Tip 2*).
   - Pick 2–3 toms maximum rather than sprawling 6-tom synth runs (*Inglis Tip 6*); do not fire repeated loud crashes on the exact same cymbal rapidly without alternating cymbals (*Inglis Tip 2*).
   - Open hi-hat build-ups should transition smoothly from tight closed hits (`GM 42`) to splashier semi-open/open (`GM 46`) and close cleanly (`GM 42/44`) on target downbeats (*Inglis Tip 18* for continuous hat transitions; GM note numbers per *gm-gs-percussion-map.md*).

## 3. Velocity Contouring, Micro-Timing & "Doubling Up" (*Dow 2010*)

1. **Weeding First ("Less Is More")** (*Dow*; *Peter Gunn*; *Inglis Tip 13*):
   - Start by muting/disabling non-essential kicks (often leaving 1–2 core kicks per bar), repetitive ghost snares, and cluttering cymbals so the fundamental pocket breathes.
2. **Alternating Hand Velocities & Phrase Ramps**:
   - In 8th/16th hi-hat or ride patterns and drum rolls, alternate stronger/weaker hand velocities (`stronger lead hand, softer off-hand`) and avoid flat velocity on kicks/snares (*Dow*; *Inglis Tip 3*).
   - Build intensity naturally by ramping velocities slightly upward toward bar ends, section seams, and chorus entrances (**Global Dynamics automation**, *Dow*).
   - Rapid double strokes / rolls decay slightly in force between quick hits (*Inglis Tip 3*).
3. **Micro-Timing & Avoiding Simultaneous Zero-Offset Hits**:
   - **Never quantize simultaneous kit hits to exact tick `0`** (*Dow §4*): offset simultaneous kick/snare/cymbal hits by subtle milliseconds; a **slightly late snare** creates a laid-back ballad pocket, while a **slightly early beat 1/3** adds urgency (*Inglis Tip 11*).
   - Use **iterative quantize** (e.g., pulling loose human timing 10–50% toward grid rather than 100%) or apply subtle `1/16` / `1/8` swing selectively to hi-hats/percussion (*Dow §§2–3*).
4. **The `1 -> 2 -> 4 -> 8` "Doubling Up" Evolution** (*Dow §5*):
   - Duplicate 1 bar → 2 bars (shift one kick by `1/8` or `1/16`, unmute/add a quiet snare ghost note, vary an open hi-hat) → duplicate to 4 bars and tweak bars 3–4 → duplicate to 8 bars and end with a transition fill.
5. **Fills & Room Cohesion**:
   - When inserting a fill at the end of an 8-bar section, clear overlapping groove hits (hats/ride), match the fill's velocity range and groove timing to the main beat, and use **overhead/room ambience + natural mic bleed** (with kick & snare centered and kit panned moderately from player/listener perspective) so the kit sounds like one acoustic instrument rather than disconnected dry samples (*Dow*; *Inglis Tip 16–17*).
