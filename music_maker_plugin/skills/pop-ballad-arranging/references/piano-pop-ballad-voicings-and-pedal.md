## 1. Register Hygiene & Non-Collision Rules

1. **Comping Register Window (`C3` to `C6`)** (*Tolson p. 5; Dunscomb/de la Cerna p. 9*):
   - Keep comping voicings between **`C3` (one octave below Middle C) and `C6` (two octaves above Middle C)**.
   - **Never double the bass part** or stack close chord tones below `C3` (and keep all tensions above `F3` — 4th line of the bass clef — to avoid mud, *Felts*).
2. **Never Double Left & Right Hand Voicings** (*Tolson p. 5*):
   - Split roles cleanly rather than duplicating the exact same chord shape in both hands.
3. **Never Clash with Guitar Comping** (*US Navy pp. 143–144; Tolson p. 5*):
   - Piano and guitar share harmonic responsibility; **they must not comp simultaneously with busy competing rhythms**. Coordinate who holds pads/arpeggios vs. who rests or plays single-line counter-fills.
4. **Never Clash with Ensemble Tensions** (*Absil §2.3.1, p. 13*):
   - Respect explicit chord extensions (e.g., avoid playing a `b9` against a melody/horn `maj9`).

## 2. Chord Architecture: Basic Chord Sound & Pop Ballad Colors

### A. Basic Chord Sound (*Randy Felts, Berklee Online*)
Every chord voicing must preserve its **Basic Chord Sound** (the `3rd` defines major/minor quality; the `7th` or `6th` defines harmonic function):
- **Triads**: `3, 5` (include `1` when playing solo piano without bass)
- **6th chords**: `3, 6`
- **7th chords**: `3, 7` (omit `5` freely unless altered!)
- **Dominant `7sus4`**: `4, 7` (3rd is avoided/omitted, *US Navy p. 15*)
- **Minor `7(b5)`**: `b3, b5, b7`
- **Augmented 7th**: `3, #5, b7` | **Diminished 7th**: `b3, b5, o7`
- *Tolson (p. 5)*: **`3rd` and `7th`** are essential for harmonic identity; **`6th` and `9th`** add optimal harmonic color; `Root` and unaltered `5th` are least essential when a bassist is present.

### B. Pop & Ballad Chords (*Dave Limina, Berklee Piano Handbook, pp. 46–53; US Navy Ch. 1*)
- **Available Tensions Rule** (*US Navy Section 1 Ch. 1, pp. 11–16*): Generally any note a **whole step above a chord tone** is an available tension, whereas a half-step above is avoided (exceptions: `b9`/`b13` on altered dominants or modal diatonic tones):
  - Major7: `T9, T13` (`T#11` if diatonic/ending) | Minor7: `T11`, `T9` (if diatonic), `T13` (Dorian only; avoid `T13` on `ii-7, iii-7, vi-7`) | Dominant7: `Tb9, T9, T#9, T#11, Tb13, T13` (follow Figure 1-15 tension avoid combinations so conflicting alterations are not mixed in the same voicing).
- **Minor 7th Chords**: Used frequently in pop ballads when a darker emotional quality is needed (*Limina p. 50*).
- **`sus4` & `sus2` Resolutions**: Restful yet tense; resolve `4 -> 3` (`Csus4 -> C`) or `2 -> 3` (`Csus2 -> C`) for expressive ballad inner-voice motion (*Limina pp. 51–52*).
- **Lush `dom7sus4(9)` Slash Voicing**: Voice `C7sus4(9)` as **`Bb/C`** (`9, b7, 4` triad over `C` bass, *Limina p. 52*), and experiment with arpeggiating it across keyboard registers.

## 3. Smooth Voice Leading & Comping Discipline

1. **Five Voice-Leading Rules** (*Felts, Berklee*):
   - **Root moves by P4/P5**: Resolve `3rd -> 7th` and `7th -> 3rd` of the next chord for minimal finger motion and clear harmonic blend (*also Tolson rule #7: move the fewest fingers possible*).
   - **Root moves by unison/2nd**: Move upper voices in **parallel motion** (optionally shifting root octave).
   - **Root moves by 3rd/6th**: Use either parallel or **contrary motion** against the bass.
   - **Range Reset**: If voice leading drifts too high/low, invert upper chord tones *within the duration of a sustained chord* before continuing.
   - **Inversions**: Keep adjacent upper voices within one octave (only lowest interval may exceed an octave); avoid doubling the bass note when it is the `3rd` or `7th` in traditional textures.
2. **Comping Rhythm & Sustained Chord Motion**:
   - **Less is More** (*Tolson p. 5; US Navy p. 144*): Simple, stylistically accurate rhythms work best; **do not play every chord on every beat**.
   - **Re-voice Long Chords** (*US Navy p. 144; Tolson p. 5*): When a chord lasts for an extended bar count, change its voicing inversion or apply gentle planing/arpeggiation to sustain interest.
   - **Timbral & Solo Intro Options** (*HK EDB p. 16; Big George Pt. 1*): In ultra-intimate ballad verses, soft/felt piano tones soften hammer transients; in Chorus/Tutti, double melodic octaves or reinforce ensemble hits (`Absil §5.4` — copy ensemble voicings in both hands on tutti hits, or plug high notes into ensemble rests).

## 4. Sustain Pedal (CC#64) Rule for Ballads

- **Ballad Exception** (*Tolson p. 5; Dunscomb/de la Cerna p. 10*): While the sustain/damper pedal should generally be avoided in uptempo swing/jazz, **the sustain pedal is explicitly prescribed for ballads** ("*should only be used for ballads and special occasions*").
- **Clean Chord Changes**: Lift and re-press CC#64 cleanly on harmonic changes (and reset at bar/chord boundaries) so rich ballad resonance connects smoothly without blurring adjacent harmonies.
