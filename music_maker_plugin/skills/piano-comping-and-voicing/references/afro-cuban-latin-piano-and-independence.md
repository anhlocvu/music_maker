# Afro-Cuban, Latin Piano Comping & Cross-Rhythm Independence

> Grounded in Zachary Charles Bartholomew (*A Jazz Pianist's Guide to Developing Rhythmic Independence Through the Adaptation of Afro-Cuban Rhythms*, Univ. of Miami DMA, 2018) and Rubim de Toledo (*African Rhythm as the Foundation of Contemporary Bass Performance*, Univ. of Auckland, 2018).

---

## 1. Piano as Percussion & Clave Architecture (*Bartholomew*)

In Afro-Cuban and Latin Jazz ensembles, treat the piano first as a **tuned percussion instrument** inside the interlocking rhythm section rather than a detached European chord pad:

1. **Match Clave Type & Direction Before Composing**:
   - **Son Clave (`3-2` or `2-3`)**:
     - `3`-side hits: beat **`1`**, **`& of 2`**, beat **`4`** (tresillo `3+3+2` eighths).
     - `2`-side hits: beats **`2`** and **`3`**.
   - **Rumba Clave (`3-2` or `2-3`)**:
     - Shifts the final stroke of the `3`-side one eighth-note later onto **`& of 4`** (or late Beat 4 triplet in triple feel), adding sharper forward tension into the downbeat.
   - **Verify Phrase Alignment (`3-2` vs `2-3`)**: Every piano `montuno`, pickup, and syncopated comp hit must align *with* the active clave direction, never fighting `"crossed clave"` against the bass `tumbao` and percussion.
2. **`12/8` Abakuá Clave ↔ Jazz Swing Bridge**:
   - The **`12/8` Abakuá clave** is the 5-note **Rumba Clave realized in triple subdivision** (`12/8` or triplet `4/4`), sharing the exact triplet feel of swung jazz ride cymbals (`6/8 cowbell / 6/8 clave` adds two extra strokes to this Abakuá pattern).
   - Use this shared triple grid to transition smoothly between straight `4/4` Latin grooves and swung triplet-feel Latin-Jazz / hard-bop grooves.

## 2. Non-Overlapping Two-Hand Interlocking Rules (*Bartholomew*)

Bartholomew's core method builds clean percussive clarity by making the two hands interlock like two independent percussionists instead of slamming notes simultaneously:

1. **LH Clave vs RH Comping Timeline Exclusion Rule**:
   - Play the **2-3 or 3-2 clave** (or `cáscara` / `tresillo` / `tumbao` bass timeline when solo) in the **Left Hand** (`C3–G4` dry percussive range).
   - Layer Afro-Cuban timelines such as **`cáscara`** or **`cinquillo`** (five-note syncopated *contradanza* cell on beats `1, & of 1, & of 2, 3, 4` [`1-2-1-2-2`], or twice per `4/4` bar in 16th-note subdivision, spanning two bars across three iterations when converted to 8th-note triplets; paired with a plain answer bar as *danzón `baqueteo`*) as chordal comping shapes (`3–7` + tensions, triads, or `montuno` figures) in the **Right Hand** (`C4–C6`).
   - **Crucial Exclusion Step**: Whenever a planned RH stroke coincides rhythmically with an active LH clave hit, **omit that note from the Right Hand** so **the two hands never strike at the same instant** (only adding deliberate unison block hits intentionally for section accents).
2. **Interlocking `Montuno` Construction**:
   - Alternate syncopated broken chord attacks (`thumb / root-5th` alternating against upper `3–7–9` triad inversions, often in octaves in the RH or `1–5` octave leaps) riding over the **`bombo` (`& of 2`)** and **`ponche` (beat `4`)** harmonic anticipations of the bass `tumbao`.
   - When the chord changes on the next bar, anticipate the harmonic change on **`& of 4`** (or **beat 4**) locked directly with the bass tumbao anticipation.

## 3. Core Cross-Rhythms, Habanera & Triplet Permutations

| Pattern / Device | Metric / Subdivision Structure | Best Musical Use |
|---|---|---|
| **`Tresillo` (`3+3+2`)** | Beats `1`, `& of 2`, `4` (in 8ths per 4/4 bar) | Left-hand Latin-jazz riffs, New Orleans funk/rumba-boogie foundations, concise comping pushes |
| **`Habanera` (`ritmo de tango`)** | Beats `1`, `& of 2`, `3`, `4` (`tresillo` + backbeat `3`) | 19th-century Cuban *contradanza* LH pattern (*Saumell / San Pascual Bailón*), tango, Bolero, dembow-Pop crossover |
| **Quarter-Note Triplets** | `3` even attacks spanning `2` beats (`3:2` cross-rhythm) | Expanding phrasing tension across barlines into chorus/bridge transitions |
| **`Mangambe` Triplet Cell** | Accenting **1st + 2nd partials** of each 8th-note triplet, resting on 3rd | Rolling Afro-Cameroonian/Latin-jazz propulsion inside `12/8` or swung `4/4` |
| **`Bikutsi` Grouping of 4** | Grouping 4 consecutive 8th-note triplets across a `12/8` bar (`4:3` polymetric feel) | Modern Latin-jazz / Afro-jazz polyrhythmic solo builds and turnaround vamp excitement |

## 4. Modal / McCoy Tyner Chordal Soloing Register Shift

When shifting from accompaniment comping into high-energy **Afro-Cuban modal vamps or chordal solo sections** (*Bartholomew*):

- **Split Hands Upward Cleanly**:
  - Push **RH chordal melody lines up an octave** into radiant percussive upper territory (`C4–C6` / `C5` peaks, octaves or locked close shapes).
  - Anchor **LH strictly on open quartal (`P4`) chords** (`C3–G4`) aligned to clave offbeats, creating wide modal resonance without stepping on the acoustic or electric bass tumbao below `C3`.
- **Pedal Control in Afro-Cuban / Latin Piano**: Keep `CC64` mostly **OFF / very shallow** during crisp `montuno`, `cáscara`, and `tumbao`-locked riffs so every syncopated offbeat articulation snaps cleanly like struck percussion. Use brief half-pedal lifts only on lyrical Bolero or ballad passages.
